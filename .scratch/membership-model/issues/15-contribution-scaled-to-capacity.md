# Contribution scaled to capacity

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Assignee: Ben (claimed 2026-07-31)
Resolved: 2026-07-31
Blocked by: 01, 03

## Question

How does someone with less money get the same place as someone with more?

Created 2026-07-31, mid-grilling on [What sits behind the paywall](05-what-sits-behind-the-paywall.md),
because the answer Ben gave there was bigger than the ticket being worked and had nowhere to
live. A decision belongs in exactly one place, so it got its own.

## Resolution (2026-07-31, grilled with Ben)

**Open for all. Contribute what you can, in money or in time. Bands are labelled by what the
money lets The Harvest do, not by what the member gets.**

Ben's words, when asked what happens to the person who cannot afford it: *"we want to create a
place open for all with whatever they can afford."*

### The three parts

**1. Self-declared, no means testing.** Northey Street City Farm runs $15/$30/$60/$125 a year
on self-declared income bands with nobody checking (ticket 01). The declaration is the
mechanism. Verification would cost more than it saves and would humiliate the people it
screens.

**2. Bands describe what the money does, not what you receive.** School Farm CSA runs four
prices for an *identical* product, each band labelled by what it enables the farm to do. This
is the part that carries the most weight, and it is easy to lose in implementation. Four prices
for the same thing, explained by purpose, is a different object from four tiers with different
perks. The second is a pricing table; the first is an invitation.

**3. Time is legal tender.** "Whatever they can afford" extends to people whose capacity is
hours rather than dollars. Money and effort are two forms of the same contribution, not a main
door and a side door. What effort buys and how it is recorded stays with
[Effort as currency](06-effort-as-currency.md), which this now blocks.

### Why this is a spine decision and not a pricing detail

It dissolves the question that was being asked when it surfaced.

The grilling on ticket 05 was working toward an acquisition perk: something tangible enough to
make someone join. The whole analysis rested on perceived value against cost, where a discount
transfers value roughly 1:1 and is therefore a poor perk, while coffee at 7x and information at
infinite are good ones.

**Under band-labelling, perks stop being the product.** If someone joins because of what their
money lets the place do, there is no acquisition hook to design, and the ratio table becomes a
second-order concern about what to include rather than the basis of the offer. That is a
different membership, and it is closer to what the site already claims to be.

It is also consistent with what ticket 01 found and ticket 03 decided. Retention rides on
belonging rather than discount. Membership funds belonging; facilities fund the place.

### What this does NOT decide

- The bands themselves, or any number ([Price and unit](07-price-and-unit.md))
- Whether there is a floor below which someone pays nothing at all
- What effort buys, at what rate, and how it is recorded (06)
- What is gated in the first place (05)
- Whether the paid tier carries a food discount (05, and still blocked on measurement)

### The honest caveat recorded at the time

Asked whether the numbers underpinning any of this were real, and how many of the 188 come
weekly, Ben answered "we don't know" to both. That is why 05 and 07 are now blocked on
[Can member visits be measured](13-can-member-visits-be-measured.md). This ticket resolves a
values-and-shape question that does not depend on those figures. Every ticket that does depend
on them stays shut until they exist.
