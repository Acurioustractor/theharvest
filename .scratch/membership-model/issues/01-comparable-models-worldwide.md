# Comparable membership models worldwide

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: —

## Question

How have comparable places around the world structured paid membership, and how do they
present it?

Look for places whose shape rhymes with The Harvest: community farms and market gardens,
community saunas and bathhouses, maker spaces and men's sheds, community-owned pubs and
shops, arts and residency spaces, and rural or village hubs that combine several of these.
Prefer small and rural over large and urban.

For each, surface:
- The tier structure. One tier or several, free tier or not, and what separates them.
- What is actually gated versus what stays open to anyone.
- Price, unit (weekly, monthly, annual, per-visit), and any household, concession or local
  resident rate. Convert to AUD and note the year.
- Whether effort, volunteering or work contribution earns access or discount, and how that
  is tracked without heavy admin.
- How capacity-limited facilities (a sauna, a workshop, a kiln) are rationed among members.
- How they present it publicly: the words used, whether price leads or follows the story,
  how the community claim is kept credible alongside a paywall. Capture verbatim wording
  worth stealing.
- Anything that visibly failed, was walked back, or drew community backlash.

Bias toward primary sources: the organisation's own pricing page, annual report, or
founder write-up, over listicles and blog summaries. Cite every source with a URL and note
the date accessed. Where a figure cannot be sourced, say so rather than estimating.

Write findings to `.scratch/membership-model/research/01-comparable-models.md`.

## Answer

Full findings: [`research/01-comparable-models.md`](../research/01-comparable-models.md)
16 organisations, 6 recurring models, all cited, FX at the 2026-07-28 ECB rate.

**The finding that matters most: no comparable organisation gives its scarce, staffed facility
away free to members.** Community Sauna Baths in London is a not-for-profit CIC whose stated
mission is access, and it still charges members for every session, merely halving the price
(GBP 23/month, about A$44). This is direct evidence against the opening position that members
use the sauna free while others pay.

**A sauna visit is a real product at a real price.** Blue Mountains Sauna in Leura charges A$45
off-peak and A$55 peak. Free member use at one visit a fortnight is over A$1,100 a year of
foregone revenue per member.

**The affordable version is a bounded allowance, and the cap is the product.** Blue Mountains
sells sessions-per-week tiers (A$32 to A$91/week, enforced with Punchpass). Community Sauna
caps its cheap unlimited tier at off-peak plus two bookings a day.

**Two Australian structural twins**
- CERES Community Environment Park gates nothing physical. A$66/year ($30 concession) buys 10%
  off enterprises plus a vote, while "CERES is a public park that is free to visit" sits in the
  header of every page, including the pricing page.
- Northey Street City Farm separates the three things this map is tempted to bundle:
  self-declared income-banded membership ($15/$30/$60/$125, no means testing), a separate
  per-bed allotment rental at cost ($208 concession / $312 general plus bond), and free land
  access for everyone. The scarce asset is a product, not a perk.

**The documented walk-back, and the closest warning for our 188.** Nottingham Hackspace ran
thirteen years of member-set fees, breached the VAT threshold in 2021, imposed a GBP 5/month
minimum in October 2023, and had 148 of roughly 650 memberships revoked overnight. Almost
nobody cited affordability as the reason. Two direct reads: expect similar attrition, and
settle the entity and GST question before a deadline forces it.

**Mechanisms worth stealing outright**
- Swansea Hackspace: "Attendance at the regular open nights is without charge and open to all."
  One named recurring free session, stated on the pricing page, costs nothing and stops the
  paywall from eating the community claim. Feeds [What stays free forever](04-what-stays-free-forever.md).
- Stearns Farm CSA publishes a buyout rate for unworked hours (USD 12/hr, about A$17). That
  makes a contribution requirement enforceable without anyone having to enforce it, and supplies
  an exchange rate we would otherwise have to invent. Feeds [Effort as currency](06-effort-as-currency.md).
- Book shifts, do not count hours. Stearns, Beardsley and Sutton all do this; Sutton uses Three
  Rings rota software. This is what keeps sweat equity cheap to administer.
- School Farm CSA's sliding scale: four prices, identical product, each band labelled by what
  the money lets the farm do rather than what the buyer gets. Self-assessed, no means test, no
  resentment. Feeds [Price and unit](07-price-and-unit.md) and [How it is presented](12-how-it-is-presented.md).
- Local-resident concessions are simple and real. Community Sauna's Stratford Locals rate is
  "anyone with proof of living in E15 and E20", six months validity, proof of address plus photo
  ID.

**Community share offers raise capital, not operating income.** Fordhall (GBP 50/share, 8,000
holders) and The Old Forge (GBP 320,000) confer ownership and a vote but no discount and no
access. Australian equivalents go deep rather than wide: Grong Grong's Royal Hotel sold $5,000
shares to 169 holders (secondary source). Recorded as fog, not as part of this map.

**The combination the research recommends:** cheap self-declared banded membership, a bounded
sauna allowance plus a member rate, one named free session forever, and the sauna, workshops
and hire priced separately as the earning products they are.

**Honest gaps.** No published Australian rural community sauna membership was found, so the
closest Australian sauna comparison is a Blue Mountains tourist-adjacent operation rather than a
village hub. No concession rate exists anywhere on Blue Mountains Sauna. The Grong Grong figure
is secondary-sourced. Full list in the "What I could not source" section of the findings file.
