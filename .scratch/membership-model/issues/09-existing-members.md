# The people already on the list

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: 03, 07

## Question

What happens to the roughly 188 existing free members and the wider 207-person newsletter
list when a paid tier arrives?

These people joined something described as free. Whatever happens next is the first thing the
model does in public, and it sets the tone.

Press on:
- Grandfathered permanently, grandfathered for a period, or asked to convert?
- What are they told, and by whom? This is a message from Ben and Nicholas, not an automated
  upgrade prompt.
- Is there a founding-member idea worth using here — early supporters locked at a rate, named
  somewhere, or given something that later members cannot get?
- What is the acceptable loss? Some will leave. Which number would mean this was handled badly?
- How does this interact with the Mighty list and the GHL tags, given membership and newsletter
  currently dual-tag on a shared ACT location?
- Does anyone on that list have a reasonable expectation of free access that predates all of
  this, and would be wronged by a paywall?

## Context from resolved tickets

From [AU recurring billing](02-au-recurring-billing-platforms.md): there is no payment
migration problem here. The 188 are free members with no card stored, so nothing has to be
moved. The real risk is list fracture across Mighty, GHL and whichever system bills, so this
ticket should also name one system as source of truth. GHL is the obvious candidate.

From [Comparable models](01-comparable-models-worldwide.md): Nottingham Hackspace is the closest
documented precedent. Thirteen years of member-set fees, then a GBP 5/month minimum in October
2023, and 148 of roughly 650 memberships revoked overnight. Almost nobody cited affordability.
Expect comparable attrition on the 188 and decide in advance what number would mean this was
handled badly.
