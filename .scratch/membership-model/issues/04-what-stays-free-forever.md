# What stays free forever

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: 03

## Question

What can anyone in Witta do at The Harvest, without paying and without joining, permanently?

Settle this as a values boundary before pricing, so price is drawn against a fixed line
rather than the line being whatever is left after pricing.

Press on:
- Walking in, being in the garden, using the grounds. Is public access unconditional?
- Community days, shared meals, the weekend pizza rhythm. Free, paid, or member-first?
- Is there a promise here The Harvest would not break even if money were tight? Name it, and
  be willing to have it quoted back.
- Where does this boundary come from — the lease, the co-design ethos, the Butterfly or DGR
  relationship, or Ben and Nicholas's own line? Different sources bind differently.
- What would have to go wrong for this boundary to move, and who gets to move it?

## Context from resolved tickets

From [Comparable models](01-comparable-models-worldwide.md): two mechanisms make this concrete.
Swansea Hackspace states on its pricing page that "Attendance at the regular open nights is
without charge and open to all" — one named recurring free session, held forever. CERES keeps
"CERES is a public park that is free to visit" in the header of every page including the pricing
page. Both cost nothing and stop a paywall from eating the community claim. Consider naming one
recurring free session as the answer to this ticket rather than a general principle.

From [One tier or two](03-one-tier-or-two.md): this ticket now has a named job. Ben committed
to a **floor** — a set of things free always includes, which does not get thinned to drive
conversion, because free governs when free and funnel conflict. Specifying that floor IS this
ticket.

Two constraints inherited:
- The cut is **marginal cost**, not perceived value. Anything with zero cost per use is a
  candidate for the floor and can be given without limit. Anything carrying a dollar figure
  every time it is used is not, and belongs in 05.
- The floor must be quotable. It gets committed to publicly and has to survive being read
  back during a tight month.
