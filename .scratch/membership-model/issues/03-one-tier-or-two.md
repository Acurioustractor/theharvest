# One tier or two

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Assignee: Ben (claimed 2026-07-31)
Resolved: 2026-07-31
Blocked by: 01

## Question

Does a free tier survive alongside the paid one, or does paid replace free entirely?

This is the spine of the map. Everything downstream — what is gated, what a Harvest Note
becomes, what happens to the existing members — changes shape depending on the answer.

Press on:
- If free survives, what is it actually for? A list of 200 people who receive notes and never
  pay is either a genuine community promise or an audience being warmed up. Which is it, and
  is Ben comfortable saying that out loud?
- If paid replaces free, what happens to the public claim of being a community garden open to
  the town? Does "member" then only mean "customer"?
- Does the word "member" survive covering both? Two relationships under one word is how this
  got confusing in the first place.
- What is the smallest number of tiers that works? Every extra tier is a page of explanation
  and a support burden for two founders, one of whom is overseas until 15 August.

## Resolution (2026-07-31, grilled with Ben)

**Two tiers. Free survives permanently. The line is drawn by marginal cost.**

This ticket settles STRUCTURE and the CUTTING PRINCIPLE only. What actually sits on each
side is tickets 04 and 05, which this unblocks. Nothing below decides a content item.

### 1. Free survives, and it is permanent

Not close, on the evidence:
- Nottingham Hackspace lost 148 of ~650 members overnight to a fee floor (ticket 01). It is
  the nearest structural analogue on record.
- 188 people joined under a free promise, before there was much here to join.
- The public copy claims a community garden open to the town; the map already holds this as a
  values boundary rather than a pricing residual.
- Free costs nothing to run. The perks it carries are zero marginal cost by construction, so
  there is no financial argument for charging for them.

The stated tension from 29 July ("as long as membership is free, the paying group is empty by
construction") is real but is NOT an argument against free existing. It is an argument about
where the boundary sits, which is 04 and 05.

### 2. The cut is marginal cost, not perceived value

- **Zero marginal cost** perks cost nothing per use and scale without limit. Free forever.
- **Real marginal cost** perks carry a dollar figure every single time they are used. Paid.

Measured, not asserted: the current offer of 15% off plus a free coffee costs **$4.72 to
$7.86 per member visit** (see asset below). That is why the cut is drawn here and not by
feel. The principle survives argument, which matters because it has to be defended in public.

### 3. Effort is a route in, not a third tier

Work earns the paid tier for a period. Same entitlements, same explanation, different
currency. A third tier is a third column on a pricing page and a third rule to enforce with
one founder overseas until 15 August. Ticket 06 now inherits a narrower question: not
"whether", but what effort buys and at what rate.

### 4. A self-declared band is one tier, not more

Northey Street runs $15/$30/$60/$125 on self-declaration with no means testing. A sliding
price is one tier. This buys the access and equity story without the explanation burden, and
it means "two tiers" can absorb concessions without becoming three. Bands themselves are
ticket 07.

### 5. Free governs when free and funnel conflict

Both things are true at once: free is a genuine community promise AND it is how people find
their way to paid. The decision is which one wins under pressure. **Free wins.**

The concrete form is a **floor**: a named set of things free always includes, which does not
get thinned to drive conversion. Ticket 04 specifies it. Two reasons this is the right call
and neither is sentiment:
- A funnel-shaped free tier creates a standing incentive to degrade it, returning every time
  conversion dips. In a town this size that is visible, and people notice a Note getting thinner.
- It is the cheapest promise available. Holding the line costs nothing, so thinning it buys
  only conversion pressure that a better paid offer would buy instead.

### Deliberately NOT decided here

- Which specific perks land on which side (04, 05)
- Whether "member" survives as one word for both relationships (still fog on the map)
- Any price, band, or number (07)
