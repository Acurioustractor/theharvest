# Effort as currency

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: 03, 15

## Question

Can showing up earn what money otherwise buys, and if so, how is it tracked without becoming
a second job?

This is the option that fits the co-design ethos better than anything else on the site: kids
build the kids area, artists shape the art space, and members use the sauna because they
helped build the place that has one. It is also the option most likely to collapse into
admin.

Press on:
- What counts. Work days only, or skills, materials, hosting, repair, teaching?
- What does an hour buy? A month of access, a session, a discount? Or is it not a rate at all,
  and simply a different door in?
- Who decides someone has done enough, and how is that not awkward between neighbours?
- How is it recorded? If the answer is "Ben remembers", it fails the moment Ben is overseas.
- Does it create two classes of member who resent each other — those who pay and those who
  turn up?
- Is there prior art from the comparable-models research worth copying outright?

## Context from resolved tickets

From [Comparable models](01-comparable-models-worldwide.md): sweat equity stays cheap to
administer only if you BOOK SHIFTS rather than count hours (Stearns, Beardsley, Sutton all do
this; Sutton uses Three Rings rota software). Stearns Farm also publishes a buyout rate for
unworked hours (USD 12/hr, about A$17), which makes a contribution requirement enforceable
without anyone having to enforce it and supplies an exchange rate we would otherwise invent.

From [One tier or two](03-one-tier-or-two.md): the "whether" is settled and this ticket is
narrower than when it was written. **Effort is a route into the paid tier, not a tier of its
own.** Same entitlements, same explanation, different currency. So the remaining questions are
what counts, what an hour buys, and how it is recorded — not whether it creates a third class
of member, which was ruled out to keep the pricing page to two columns.

~~That also gives the exchange rate a fixed point... roughly 15 hours.~~ **CORRECTED 2026-07-31
by ticket 16.** That estimate used the Stearns A$17/hr figure, which turns out to be the TOP of the
range, not the middle. Real Australian rates: Essendon Community Gardens $8.33/hr, Lorne SLSC
~$3.13/hr. Against a ~$256/yr floor that is **31 to 82 hours a year**, not 15. The design question
is unchanged (too cheap and money stops being worth paying; too dear and effort is decoration) but
the numbers are very different and 82 hours is a different proposition from 15.

## Grilling paused 2026-07-31, pending research

Claimed and worked. Stalled on the first question, which is the fork everything else hangs off:

**Is effort a RATE (hours convert to dollars at a published exchange, creating a ledger) or a
DOOR (turning up is membership, no accounting)?** A third shape was on the table: the door as the
unit, with a published buyout rate existing only so that not turning up has an answer other than
an awkward conversation between neighbours.

The argument assembled for the door: the ticket itself says a scheme fails the moment it depends
on Ben remembering, and he is away annually; ticket 01 found that real schemes book shifts rather
than count hours; and an hourly exchange rate reinstalls on labour exactly the transactional
ledger that ticket 15 deliberately removed from money.

**Paused rather than decided**, because that argument rests on four data points, three of them
Northern Hemisphere, and the question is operational rather than a matter of judgement. Raised
[How sweat equity actually works in practice](16-how-sweat-equity-actually-works.md), which now
blocks this ticket. Resume here once it reports, with the rate-versus-door fork as question one.

## Research landed 2026-07-31 — read before resuming

[How sweat equity actually works in practice](16-how-sweat-equity-actually-works.md) is resolved.
Full findings in `research/03-sweat-equity-in-practice.md`. Three things change this ticket.

**1. This ticket's own assumption was backwards.** It asks whether effort "creates two classes of
member who resent each other, those who pay and those who turn up". Every scheme found says no.
The real fracture is between reliable and unreliable *workers*. Park Slope's manual, explaining why
it doubles the make-up penalty: *"attendance was often bad enough to cause those who did show up to
quit because of the unfair burden placed on them."* Design against people carrying each other's
shifts, not against payers resenting workers.

**2. Nobody chases anyone.** Not one Australian scheme found chases a no-show. They enforce by
lapse (the discount simply stops), renewal surcharge, eligibility gate, or self-substitution
(you find your own replacement). The awkward-conversation-between-neighbours problem is solved by
never having the conversation. That strengthens the DOOR side of the fork considerably.

**3. The buyout exists in Australia, but gated rather than self-serve.** The Food Co-op Canberra
requires *written board approval* at an AGM-set price, with the board weighing "family
circumstances, equity and capacity". A published self-serve tariff was the shape assumed when this
ticket paused; the nearest comparable deliberately does not do that, because a self-serve price
lets anyone opt out of community with money, which is the thing the scheme exists to prevent.

**Also, and bigger than this ticket:** WHS Act 2011 (Qld) s7(1)(h) makes volunteers workers, and
the volunteer-association carve-out fails once you employ anyone. The Harvest employs and already
runs work days, so this duty exists **today**, whatever this map decides. Carried to the insurance
call sheet. It is a fixed cost of having people work on site, not a variable this ticket can trade.
