# What sits behind the paywall

Type: grilling
Status: open
Blocked by: 03, 04

## Question

Of the sauna, workshops and talks, the building's inside spaces, space hire and the garden,
which are gated, which are discounted, and which are open to all?

Ben's starting position (2026-07-29): members get the sauna free, others pay. Test it, and
test each other facility separately rather than assuming one rule fits all.

Press on:
- Facility by facility: gated, discounted, or open. Do not let one answer stand in for all.
- Is the gate binary, or is it a bounded free allowance — a session a month, off-peak only,
  first hour?
- Which of these could carry the place financially if it were sold rather than given? That is
  the real cost of gating it to members.
- Does gating something change what it is? A community sauna and a members' sauna are
  different objects with the same timber.
- What does a non-member actually see when they arrive and want in? The answer has to work at
  a gate on a cold Saturday, not just on a pricing page.

## Context from resolved tickets

From [Comparable models](01-comparable-models-worldwide.md): the evidence is against a binary
gate on the sauna. No comparable organisation gives a scarce staffed facility away free to
members. The recurring affordable pattern is a bounded allowance where the cap is the product
(sessions per week, or off-peak plus a daily booking limit), with a member rate rather than free
entry. Northey Street City Farm separates membership, the scarce asset (allotment beds, rented
at cost), and free land access into three distinct things. Test that separation here: membership,
facility, and open access may not want to be the same decision.

From [One tier or two](03-one-tier-or-two.md): the sorting rule is settled — real marginal
cost sits on the paid side. What is new is that the numbers exist, so "which are gated" can be
argued with figures rather than instinct:

| item | keeps at full price | keeps at the member rate |
|---|---|---|
| DIY pizza | $18.79 | $14.77 |
| Sauna session | $36.19 | not modelled, sauna does not exist |

The food and drink discount alone costs **$4.02 a visit**, and the free coffee $0.70 to $3.85
depending on whether they would have bought one. The sauna is the highest-contribution item in
the entire model at $36.19 a session, which sharpens this ticket's own question about which
facility could carry the place if it were sold rather than given.

The live question this ticket must now answer: **does the paid tier carry a food and drink
discount at all?** It is the most expensive perk per use, it is the one that inverts against
frequent members, and dropping it would move the price floor from ~$256/yr to close to
nothing. That is a real option, not a rhetorical one.
