## TELL US WHO YOU ARE (max 2,400 characters)

```
We are Ben Knight and Nicholas — co-founders of The Harvest, a community space forming at 9 Gumland Drive, Witta, on the Blackall Range in the Sunshine Coast Hinterland, on Jinibara Country.

Our creative practice sits at the intersection of community cultural development, placemaking, and cross-disciplinary art. We work with food, soil, story, and making — connecting the heritage of this ridge (timber, dairy, and the cooperative movement) to contemporary creative practice.

Ben is a designer and technologist who builds community infrastructure. Nicholas brings vision, spatial design, and a background in experiential hospitality — his principle is radical simplicity: "If you do a bit of everything, what is it?" This discipline shapes everything we make.

Together we are Residency No. 1 at The Harvest — the founding creative residents whose work establishes the model for all future seasons. Our current project, Radical Scoops (funded by Regional Arts Australia, $45,000), explores the dairy, timber, and cooperative heritage of the Blackall Range through photography, oral histories, and recycled material structures. We have completed a photographic portrait series with Barry Rodgerig (our neighbour since 1972), and in March 2026 we built the Milk Crate Pavilion — a community-constructed structure made from donated dairy industry crates — with 80+ locals at our First Gathering.

Our team includes Sophie (producer, full-time), Susie (operations), Cath (NDIS-accessible garden design), Thais (architect, rammed earth studio design), and Leah (kids program lead). We work with Shaun Fisher, a Quandamooka oyster farmer and our first cross-cultural collaborator.

The Harvest sits in a region with 157 creative arts workers but no dedicated community gallery space. Maleny has strong creative infrastructure (Arts Council, Music Festival, Singers) but Witta — five minutes up the road — has no public gathering space at all. We are building one, and the gallery is its cultural heart.
```

## WHAT DO YOU WANT TO USE THE FUNDING FOR? (max 5,600 characters)

```
This funding activates The Harvest Art Space — a community gallery, seasonal residency, and creative engagement program inside a rammed earth building on a 5-acre former nursery site in Witta, Sunshine Coast Hinterland, on Jinibara Country.

THE SPACE

The gallery is the interior of a rammed earth building with timber floors, a raised stage at the back, and a fireplace in the centre. Round tables create a warm, cooperative feel. A residency workspace occupies a private room at the rear. An outdoor studio (covered pergola with rammed earth elements, designed by architect Thais) provides space for large-scale, messy making. The gallery connects through garden paths to an outdoor sculpture trail.

The core principle is transformation: everything can be removed between seasons. Gallery one quarter, theatre the next, ceramics workshop after that. The space is always becoming.

WHAT WE WILL DO

We will deliver four seasonal cycles across 12 months (January 2027 — December 2027), each following the same structure. By the time this activity begins, The Harvest will already be 6 months into active trading (commenced 1 July 2026 under our community lease) with programming, garden, and a values-aligned café sub-operator in place. The gallery activation builds on a living site, not an empty one.

Season 1 (Jan–Mar 2027): LAUNCH
— First exhibition installed. Open call for Season 2 resident artist.
— Community co-design workshops to establish gallery programming (drawing on 6 months of community engagement data from the site's trading operations).
— Kids design workshop: children shape their creative area for the season.
— Open studio weekends (monthly) where visitors watch artists work.
— Launch event: curated dining experience in the gallery ($30/head, 40 seats) combining art, food from the garden, and live performance.

Season 2 (Apr–Jun 2027): GROW
— Resident artist begins 3-month residency (artist, paired with seasonal gardener and chef).
— Exhibition changeover — resident curates what goes on the walls.
— Community workshops led by resident (skill-sharing, open to all).
— Kids redesign their area.
— End-of-residency public talk and open studio.
— Second curated dining event.
— Coincides with Harvest's first-option lease renewal decision (30 Jun 2027) — gallery programme strengthens the community-value case for lease continuation.

Season 3 (Jul–Sep 2027): MAKE
— New resident artist. Gallery transforms — possibly theatre configuration or ceramics workshop.
— Outdoor sculpture trail installed along garden paths.
— School group visits begin (farm-to-plate + art education).
— Maker market under the pavilion (local makers test ideas, find audiences).
— Third curated dining event.

Season 4 (Oct–Dec 2027): HARVEST
— Final resident of the funded year. Gallery exhibition.
— Community reflection event: what worked, what next, who leads.
— Documentation exhibition: photography, oral histories, and process work from all four seasons compiled as a community archive.
— Handover to community governance body (body form decided by March 2027 per Harvest's internal governance plan; first meeting target June 2027; by Dec 2027 the body is in its second operating quarter and ready to take leadership of the gallery programme).

HOW WE WILL DO IT

Each season follows a repeating cycle:
1. Open call for resident artist (national, with priority for regional Queensland artists)
2. Resident arrives, meets gardener and chef, begins making
3. Community co-design workshops shape that season's programming
4. Exhibition/installation opens with a curated community event
5. Open studios, workshops, and school visits run throughout
6. End-of-season public talk, documentation, changeover

Cross-pollination is central: the chef works with the gardener on what to grow, the artist responds to both. The gallery menu changes with the exhibition. The kids area evolves each season. Nothing is fixed.

WHY NOW

We are not speculating about what this site could be — we are already operating on it. The timeline:

- March 2026: Stage 1 activation. First Gathering — 80+ locals building the Milk Crate Pavilion, sharing food, hearing oral histories of the ridge.
- February 2026: Landlord-signed 11-year lease executed for 9 Gumland Drive, Witta — a community-purpose lease with a $250,000 landlord-funded Capital Improvement Fund.
- July 2026: Lease commences. Harvest begins active trading as a site-steward: programming, Art Space preparation, garden development, and sourcing of a values-aligned café sub-operator to activate daily hospitality.
- July–December 2026: First 6 months of active operations. Community embedded, programming rhythm established, first quarterly open-book transparency report published, café sub-operator trading.
- Late 2026 (if this grant lands): Gallery activation becomes the next layer — Stage 3 — on a site already proven and held by community relationships.

By the time Round 2 assessors read this application in September–November 2026, The Harvest will have been trading for 3–5 months. This is not an application about what we hope to build. It is an application to deepen what we have already started.

We are not starting from scratch. The building exists. The lease is signed. The community is engaged. The team is in place. The Radical Scoops fellowship (Regional Arts Australia, $45,000) funded the foundational creative research. This grant funds the leap from research to gallery program — from a trading community hub into a sustained, seasonal creative infrastructure that the community can eventually run themselves.

Leah (kids program, quarterly co-design workshops)
```

## IMPACT (max 2,400 characters)

```
This funding creates the only community-led gallery and artist residency program in the Blackall Range hinterland — a region with 157 creative arts workers but no dedicated exhibition or residency space.

CREATIVE IMPACT
Four seasonal resident artists will develop new work in a cross-disciplinary environment where art, food, and gardening intersect. The residency model — pairing an artist with a gardener and a chef each season — produces work that couldn't exist in a conventional gallery. The chef works with the gardener on what to grow; the artist responds to both. This cross-pollination generates genuinely original creative outcomes.

COMMUNITY IMPACT
The co-design methodology means the community shapes every season. Kids design their own creative area — not a playground installed for them, but a space they build and rebuild each quarter. Local makers test ideas at seasonal markets. Community members choose workshop topics. The governance handover in the final season ensures the program outlives the grant — this isn't a one-year project, it's the launch of a permanent community cultural asset.

REGIONAL IMPACT
Witta has no public gathering space. The nearest gallery is in Maleny (10 minutes away) and operates a traditional exhibition model. The Harvest Art Space offers something different: a transforming space where the program changes every season, where making happens in public view, and where food and art are inseparable. This model — seasonal, community-led, cross-disciplinary — is replicable across regional Australia.

INCLUSION AND ACCESS
NDIS-accessible garden design (by Cath) integrates therapeutic horticulture with the creative program. Curated dining events are priced accessibly ($30/head). Open studios and community workshops are free. The kids program works with both local schools and homeschool families. The sliding-scale principle runs through everything we do.

FIRST NATIONS
The Harvest sits on Jinibara Country. We work with Shaun Fisher (Quandamooka) as our first cross-cultural collaborator and adhere to Creative Australia's First Nations Cultural and Intellectual Property Protocols. Future residency seasons will include dedicated First Nations artist residencies.
```
