# Demographics & Population

**Geographic context**: This chapter draws on two datasets at different geographies. Population estimates use the ABS Estimated Resident Population (ERP) for the **Maroochy Hinterland SA2** (316061443), a tighter boundary around Witta and surrounds. Census data uses **POA 4552**, the postcode area covering Maleny, Witta, and surrounding localities -- a larger catchment that includes the Maleny township. Keep this mismatch in mind when comparing figures across tables.

This is a slow, steady growth curve -- not a boom-bust pattern -- which signals a community that absorbs newcomers gradually.

> [!insight] Median age 56 -- a retiree-heavy community
> Over half the population (52.7%) is aged 55 or older. The 65--74 bracket alone accounts for one in five residents. Meanwhile, 20--34 year olds represent just 8.6% of the population -- a "missing generation" pattern typical of hinterland communities where young adults leave for education and employment in coastal or metro centres. The 0--14 cohort (12.5%) signals a meaningful family presence, but one that is clearly outnumbered by retirees.

> [!insight] Programming implications for The Harvest
> The age profile points to two primary audience segments: **(1) active retirees** (55--74) with time, skills, and moderate disposable income, and **(2) young families** (parents 35--44 with children 0--14). Programs should serve both -- but the sheer numbers favour retiree engagement as the volume driver. Youth programs (15--24) serve a small but high-impact cohort with few existing outlets.

## Income & Housing (Census 2021, POA 4552)

| Indicator | Value |
|-----------|------:|
| Median personal income | $631/week |
| Median household income | $1,259/week |
| Median family income | $1,498/week |
| Median rent | $370/week |
| Median mortgage repayment | $1,700/month |
| Average household size | 2.3 persons |
| Average persons per bedroom | 0.7 |

> [!insight] Modest incomes, high housing costs
> Median personal income of $631/week ($32,812/year) sits well below the national median, consistent with a retirement-heavy population drawing pensions and superannuation. Yet median mortgage repayments of $1,700/month reflect Sunshine Coast property values that have surged. The gap between income and housing cost is the defining affordability tension in this community -- and a key reason younger demographics struggle to stay.

> [!insight] Small households, spacious homes
> Average household size of 2.3 and just 0.7 persons per bedroom indicate a community of couples and small households in houses built for families. This "underoccupied housing" pattern is common in ageing hinterland areas and has implications for housing policy, home-share programs, and the type of gathering spaces people seek out.

> [!question] Enrichment prompt
> What share of dwellings are owned outright vs. mortgaged vs. rented? Tenure type strongly correlates with community stability and willingness to invest in local initiatives. Renters in the hinterland are often in a precarious position given low supply and rising prices.

## Key Takeaways for The Harvest

1. **Volume audience is 55+**: Any program that ignores retirees is ignoring the majority. But retirees here are often highly educated, skilled, and seeking purpose -- not passive consumers.

2. **Young families are the growth edge**: The 35--44 parent cohort with children is present and likely growing with the tree-change trend. Family-friendly programming creates intergenerational bridges.

3. **Youth gap is real**: The 20--34 cohort is thin. The Harvest can't solve the structural reasons young people leave, but it can create reasons to stay or return -- apprenticeships, creative residencies, remote work infrastructure.

4. **Income-sensitive pricing matters**: With median personal income around $630/week, pricing for workshops, produce, and events must be accessible. Sliding scale, work-exchange, and volunteer-for-access models align with both the economic reality and the community ethos.

5. **Steady growth, not explosive**: The 1--2% annual growth rate means The Harvest can plan for gradual scaling rather than boom capacity. New arrivals need onboarding into community -- The Harvest can be that front door.
