# Community Personas & Needs Report
**The Harvest, Witta — Jinibara Country**
*February 2026 | Built from 14 research datasets*

---

## The Community at a Glance

**9,019 people** in postal area 4552 (Maleny/Witta). Median age **56**. A tree-change community of teachers, health workers, artists, and landcarers. Values-driven but cash-constrained ($1,259/week household income). 72% Australian-born, 156 First Nations people (Jinibara Country).

**What unites them:** Local food, environmental stewardship, community spirit, resistance to homogenisation.

**What divides them:** Long-timers vs newcomers, tourism-supporters vs tourism-resisters, those who own land vs those priced out.

---

## Part 1: What the Community Loves, Wants, Hates & Dreams Of

### LOVES

**Food that has a name attached to it**
> "Jim has been selling veges since time immemorial. He's always friendly and up for a quick chat."
— Google Review, Witta Markets

People don't just want fresh food — they want to *know the person who grew it*. The relational economy is alive here. Jim isn't a vendor, he's a neighbour.

> "I love this place. Their range, location, staff, pricing, atmosphere. Shopping here is like therapy."
— Google Review, Maple Street Co-op

The Co-op isn't a shop. It's a *place of belonging*. 20+ years of relationship. This is what The Harvest needs to become for space and gathering.

**Nature as identity, not amenity**
77 mentions of nature/environment across Google Reviews. But it's not "nice scenery" — it's identity. People *moved here for this*. Platypus in the creek. Koalas in the corridor. The Dark Sky Reserve got 95% support from 1,200 respondents.

> "Beautiful little creek... Definitely worth checking out."
— Gardners Falls review

**Hands-on making, not passive watching**
> "No one is 'left behind', ever."
— Review of Maleny Printmakers workshop

People want to *do* things, not observe. The Maleny Wood Expo, Scarecrow Festival, and printmaking workshops all confirm: participation > spectatorship.

**Cooperative economics**
Maple Street Co-op, Maleny Credit Union, Crystal Waters — this community has a 40-year tradition of *owning things together*. The cooperative model isn't alternative here, it's mainstream.

---

### WANTS (What's Missing)

**A gathering space that isn't a business**
Restaurants = commercial. Co-op = retail. Churches = declining. Library = tiny ("It's a tad small, but, well, where would a suitable relocation be?"). There is no dedicated **community gathering space** for shared meals, making, and growing — together, affordably, regularly.

Council is *explicitly asking* for this in multiple consultations (Community Strategy, Recreation Parks Plan, Neighbourhood Projects — which is still OPEN).

**Food growing knowledge exchange**
3,000 members in Range Gardeners Facebook group. Themes: `food_sovereignty`, `knowledge_sharing`, `seasonal_growing`. People want to grow food, learn from each other, share seeds and skills. But there's no physical hub for this.

**Workshop & maker space**
Maleny Arts & Crafts (2,000 members) — themes: `workshop_demand`, `exhibition_opportunities`. Artists need studio access, exhibition space, kilns, tools. There's nowhere permanent.

**Integration pathway for newcomers**
Tree-changers join 3-4 Facebook groups to figure out the hinterland. But online ≠ belonging. They need a *physical place* where you show up, get your hands dirty, and become part of something.

**Youth-led creative space**
Recreation clubs exist, but nothing creative for young people. No teen-led maker space, no kids' co-design. 269 kids aged 0-4, 859 aged 5-14 — they need a place too.

---

### HATES (Frustrations & Fears)

**Tourism pressure**
16 mentions of tourism tension in reviews. Popular spots (Kondalilla Falls, Mary Cairncross) are overcrowded. Parking is a nightmare. People are protective:
> This isn't for the masses.

**Development eating rural character**
Dominant theme across Facebook (12,000-member Maleny Community Board), media (15 housing articles, 8 development pressure articles), and council consultations. 90 of 95 Sunshine Coast suburbs now over $1M median. People fear becoming "just another coast suburb."

**Being priced out of their own community**
Median rent was $370/week in 2021 — likely $500+ now. The Buy Sell Swap group (8,000 members) exists because people need to trade locally to survive. `affordability`, `rental_crisis`, `housing_affordability` are top Facebook sentiment themes.

**Hollow consultation without action**
Council has run 13+ consultations. People are tired of *being asked* without seeing results. The Harvest can't be another consultation — it has to *build the thing*.

**Gentrification of local culture**
When a French patisserie opens and a pizza joint calls itself "The Local" — there's a thin line between celebration and appropriation of hinterland identity. People want authenticity.

---

### DREAMS (Aspirations)

**A community resilience hub**
When ex-Cyclone Alfred dumped 750mm on the kindergarten, when roads closed for months, when bushfires threaten — people rally. 55+ SES volunteers honoured for decades of service. The dream: a *permanent place* where community resilience lives, not just emerges in crisis.

**Jinibara cultural recognition**
156 Aboriginal people in the census, but **zero** Jinibara heritage sites on the Queensland Heritage Register. SCC has a Reconciliation Action Plan calling for `first_nations_engagement`, `cultural_heritage`, `truth_telling`. The dream: a place where Jinibara story is told *first*, not as an afterthought.

**The cooperative model at landscape scale**
Crystal Waters proved intentional community works. Maple Street Co-op proved cooperative retail works. Barung Landcare proved cooperative conservation works. The dream: what if *a whole precinct* — garden, kitchen, art space — was built and run cooperatively?

**Dark sky, slow time**
95% support for Dark Sky Reserve. Platypus sighting was the #2 most-viewed video on Visit Sunshine Coast. People dream of *slowing down further* — not more activity, but deeper presence.

## Part 2: The Personas

### Persona 1: "The Steward" — Margaret, 63

**Who she is:** Semi-retired health worker. Moved from Brisbane 12 years ago. Owns 5 acres, volunteers with Barung Landcare twice a month. Grows most of her own vegetables. Member of Range Gardeners (Facebook), Maleny Community Board, and the Co-op.

**Census fit:** 34.7% of the population is 65+ (3,130 people). 378 health workers. Strong Barung/landcare volunteer base.

**What she loves:** Her morning bird calls. Saturday at Witta Markets buying Jim's veges. The sense that her volunteering *matters* — she's planted 2,000 trees.

**What she needs:** Community around food growing (not just lonely gardening). A place to share her health knowledge. Intergenerational connection — she misses her grandkids in Melbourne.

**What frustrates her:** "Another bloody subdivision." The young families who move here and don't know how to compost. Council consultations that go nowhere.

**Her dream for The Harvest:** A community garden where she can teach kids to grow beans. Wednesday morning tea with other growers. An annual seed swap. She'd be there weekly.

**How to reach her:** Barung Landcare newsletter. Range Gardeners Facebook group. Word of mouth from Witta Markets vendors. Physical flyer at Maple Street Co-op.

**How to keep her:** Give her a garden bed with her name on it. Ask her to mentor a newcomer family. Invite her to the first harvest dinner. She'll bring her friends.

---

### Persona 2: "The Maker" — Damo, 47

**Who he is:** Furniture maker and part-time carpenter. Studied Creative Arts, now does custom timber pieces from his shed. Sells at Eumundi Markets occasionally. Active in Maleny Arts & Crafts Facebook group. Lives on a half-acre in Witta with his partner and two dogs.

**Census fit:** 93 Creative Arts graduates. 414 Technicians & Trades Workers. Strong woodworking culture (Maleny Wood Expo is beloved).

**What he loves:** Working with his hands. The forestry heritage of this area — his timber comes from local mills. The quality of light in the hinterland. Community concerts at the Upfront Club.

**What he needs:** Workshop access beyond his shed (no kiln, no large format tools). A community of other makers. Exhibition opportunities that aren't tourist-trap galleries. Income stability — markets are inconsistent.

**What frustrates him:** Montville galleries selling mass-produced "handmade" goods. The cost of workshop insurance. Being seen as a hobbyist, not a professional. "I can't afford to rent a studio in Maleny anymore."

**His dream for The Harvest:** A shared maker space with a kiln, woodworking tools, and printmaking press. Monthly open studio nights. A makers' co-op where they sell direct. Teaching woodworking to teenagers.

**How to reach him:** Maleny Arts & Crafts Facebook group. Maleny Wood Expo committee. Word of mouth through Eumundi Market stallholders. A physical postcard at Black Bamboo Gallery.

**How to keep him:** Give him a residency — 3 months of studio access in exchange for running 4 workshops. Feature his work in the space. Connect him with other makers. He'll become an ambassador.

---

### Persona 3: "The Seeker" — Priya, 34

**Who she is:** Moved from Sydney 18 months ago with her partner and 3-year-old daughter. Works remotely as a UX designer. Joined 4 Facebook groups in her first week. Shops at the Co-op but doesn't know anyone by name yet. Renting — looking to buy but everything is over $1M.

**Census fit:** 500-1,000 tree-changers estimated. 404 people speak a language other than English. Young families with children = significant segment. Rental crisis is real.

**What she loves:** The quiet. The fact her daughter can play outside safely. The community markets. The *idea* of hinterland life — she just hasn't found her way in yet.

**What she needs:** A way into community that isn't intimidating. Kid-friendly activities. Other young parents. Skills she doesn't have (gardening, food preservation — she grew up in an apartment). Affordable social life.

**What frustrates her:** Feeling like an outsider. "Everyone seems to know each other already." The rental market (they've moved twice). The lack of childcare options. Driving everywhere because there's no walkable centre.

**Her dream for The Harvest:** A place she can take her daughter on Saturday mornings. A gardening mentor (like Margaret!). Meeting other young families. Learning to make sourdough. Finally feeling like she *belongs* here.

**How to reach her:** Sunshine Coast Hinterland Community Facebook group (15,000 members). Instagram (she's visual). The Witta Community Facebook group (hyper-local). QR code at Witta Markets.

**How to keep her:** Welcome her by name. Pair her with a mentor. Give her daughter a garden bed. Invite her to a community dinner where she sits next to someone who also moved here recently. Within 6 months, she'll be volunteering.

### Persona 4: "The Elder" — Barry, 76

**Who he is:** Born in Maleny. Third generation. His grandfather cleared the land. Retired dairy farmer — sold his herd 10 years ago. Active in the Witta Recreation Club and RSL. Knows everyone. Suspicious of newcomers who "want to change things."

**Census fit:** Witta Community Facebook (500 members — the hyper-locals). Multi-generational families. Agricultural heritage. 156 Aboriginal/TSI persons (Barry would know Jinibara elders personally).

**What he loves:** The Maleny Show. Jim at the markets. A beer at the golf club. The way things used to be. His grandchildren (who live in Nambour now).

**What he needs:** To feel respected, not sidelined. To know his story matters. Physical accessibility (he's had a knee replacement). His mates — half of them have moved to aged care.

**What frustrates him:** "All these blow-ins telling us what we need." Development pressure — his neighbour's farm is now a subdivision. Being made to feel like the past doesn't matter. Loneliness since his wife died.

**His dream for The Harvest:** Honestly, he doesn't dream about it. He's wary. But if you invited him to tell his story — about the dairy, the floods, the bushfires, the time the community rebuilt the hall — he'd come. And once he's there, he'll be back.

**How to reach him:** You don't reach Barry online. You reach him through the Witta Recreation Club, the RSL, the golf club, and the men who drink coffee at the Maleny bakery. Personal invitation from someone he knows.

**How to keep him:** Ask him to speak at a heritage story night. Put a photo of his grandfather's dairy in the space. Give him a comfortable chair and a cup of tea. Introduce him to Priya's daughter. He'll be her honorary grandpa within a month.

### Persona 5: "The Activist" — Jules, 52

**Who she is:** Permaculture teacher. Lives at Crystal Waters. Sits on Barung Landcare board. Runs workshops on composting and food forests. Vegan. Drives an old Subaru covered in bumper stickers. Member of 6+ Facebook groups. Knows the council planning scheme better than most councillors.

**Census fit:** 129 Agriculture/Environmental workers. Crystal Waters community (2,500 followers). Barung Landcare (3,000 followers). The "connective tissue" — estimated 500-1,000 people who bridge all the sustainability groups.

**What she loves:** That this community has a 40-year cooperative tradition. Barung's native plant nursery. When a council consultation actually leads to change. Teaching kids where food comes from.

**What she needs:** More venues for workshops. A kitchen for food preservation classes. Connection between Crystal Waters and Maleny/Witta (they're siloed). Funding pathways for her programs.

**What frustrates her:** "Council asks us what we want, then approves another subdivision." Burnout — she's on 4 committees. The people who talk about sustainability but drive SUVs. Being seen as "that hippie" instead of a professional.

**Her dream for The Harvest:** A demonstration site for regenerative agriculture. A commercial kitchen for her food preservation workshops. Indigenous food plant walks led by Jinibara elders. An annual Harvest Festival that rivals Woodford.

**How to reach her:** She's already watching. She probably knows about The Harvest. Reach her through Barung Landcare, Crystal Waters community, or direct invitation to a planning session. She needs to feel like a *co-creator*, not a customer.

**How to keep her:** Put her on the programming advisory board. Give her a regular workshop slot. Connect her with funding (SCC Arts & Heritage Levy, Community Grants). Share credit publicly. She'll bring 50 people with her.

### Persona 6: "The Struggler" — Kai, 28

**Who he is:** Works at the Maleny IGA and does landscape maintenance on weekends. Shares a rental in Landsborough with two housemates because he can't afford Maleny. Grew up here — went to Maleny State High. Most of his school friends left for Brisbane.

**Census fit:** The youngest bracket is underrepresented (only 462 people aged 20-24, 773 aged 25-34). Median personal income $631/week. Buy Sell Swap group (8,000 members) signals economic precarity. Rental crisis across all Facebook groups.

**What he loves:** The hinterland — it's home. Fishing at Lake Baroon. Music (he plays guitar, goes to every gig at the Upfront Club). His nan who still lives in Witta.

**What he needs:** Community that doesn't cost anything. Skills for better work (he'd love to do carpentry). A sense that this place has a future for him, not just for retirees and tree-changers. Affordable housing — but that's bigger than The Harvest.

**What frustrates him:** "Everything here is for tourists or rich people." Being invisible — all the community planning is done by people over 50. The 40-minute drive to his rental. Watching his hometown become unaffordable.

**His dream for The Harvest:** Free workshops where he can learn woodworking. Open mic nights. A place to hang out on Saturday that isn't the pub. Meeting people his age who stayed.

**How to reach him:** Maleny Music Festival socials. Through his nan (who knows everyone at Witta Markets). The Upfront Club gig listings. Don't use Facebook — he's on Instagram and Spotify.

**How to keep him:** Offer work-trade: help set up the garden beds, get free workshop access. Let him play at open mic night. Introduce him to Damo who needs an apprentice. Make him feel like this place is *for him too*.

## Part 3: The Engagement Flywheel

### Stage 1: ATTRACT — Getting Them Through the Door

| Persona | Channel | Message | First Touchpoint |
|---------|---------|---------|-----------------|
| **Margaret** (Steward) | Barung newsletter, Co-op noticeboard | "Bring your seeds, share your knowledge" | Monthly Garden Day |
| **Damo** (Maker) | Arts & Crafts FB, Wood Expo contacts | "A maker space for the hinterland" | Open Studio Night |
| **Priya** (Seeker) | SC Hinterland FB, Instagram, QR at markets | "Find your people. Grow something." | Saturday Family Morning |
| **Barry** (Elder) | Personal invite via Witta Rec Club | "Tell your story. We're listening." | Heritage Story Night |
| **Jules** (Activist) | Direct invitation to advisory board | "Help us design the programming" | Co-design Workshop |
| **Kai** (Struggler) | Instagram, Upfront Club gigs, word of mouth | "Free workshops. Open mic. Your place." | Open Mic Night |

### Stage 2: CONNECT — First Visit to Regular

**The "3 Visits" Rule:** If someone comes 3 times, they stay. Design for the second and third visit.

- **Visit 1:** They experience something. (A garden day. A workshop. A dinner.)
- **Visit 2:** They're invited back *personally* — by name, for something specific. ("Margaret, we're doing a tomato workshop next Saturday and we need someone who knows about heritage varieties.")
- **Visit 3:** They have a *role*. (Margaret mentors Priya. Damo teaches Kai. Barry tells stories to the kids. Jules runs the composting station.)

### Stage 3: BELONG — Regular to Community Member

**Give people ownership:**
- A garden bed with their name
- A recurring workshop they lead
- A role on a programming committee
- A story on the wall

**Create rituals:**
- Monthly Garden Day (1st Saturday — aligned with Witta Markets)
- Quarterly Feast Dinner (seasonal, farm-to-table, by donation)
- Annual Harvest Festival (autumn — the big one)
- Weekly drop-in hours (low key, just be there)

### Stage 4: ADVOCATE — Member to Ambassador

When people belong, they recruit. Margaret tells Range Gardeners. Damo brings his Eumundi Market network. Priya posts on Instagram. Barry vouches for The Harvest at the golf club. Jules connects her 6 committees. Kai tells his mates.

**The flywheel accelerates because each persona reaches a different network.**

## Part 5: What This Means for Programming

### The First 6 Events (In Order)

1. **Heritage Story Night** — Invite Barry and 5 other elders. Record their stories. Serve tea and scones. This earns legitimacy.

2. **Garden Day #1** — Margaret leads seed swap + planting. Jules does a composting demo. Priya brings her daughter. Open, free, Saturday morning.

3. **Open Maker Session** — Damo shows timber joinery. Other makers bring tools. Kai helps set up. Low key, hands-on, no pressure.

4. **Community Dinner #1** — Farm-to-table, by donation. Jim provides the veges. Seating is mixed — Barry next to Priya, Margaret next to Kai. This is where belonging happens.

5. **Kids' Co-Design Day** — Children draw/build what they want the kids' area to be. Parents meet each other. Jules runs a garden activity. Nicholas photographs it.

6. **Open Mic + Makers Market** — Kai plays. Damo sells. Local food vendors. Sunset. This becomes the recurring monthly event.
