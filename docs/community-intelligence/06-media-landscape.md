# Media Landscape & Community Narratives

**Source overview**: This chapter analyses 101 articles from local media covering the Sunshine Coast hinterland, sourced from **Sunshine Coast News** (79 articles) and **myPolice Sunshine Coast** (22 articles). Articles were coded by theme and sentiment to reveal what the region talks about, worries about, and celebrates -- and where The Harvest fits into that conversation.

See also: [[00-index]] | [[05-digital-community]] | [[11-harvest-opportunities]]

## Theme Analysis

Articles coded by dominant theme. Where articles span multiple themes, the primary theme was assigned. Sentiment reflects the prevailing tone of coverage, not the topic's inherent valence.

> [!insight] Infrastructure dominates coverage -- The Harvest can reframe the conversation toward community assets
> One third of all articles (34 of 101) focus on infrastructure complaints -- roads, services, facilities. This is the default media lens for the hinterland. The Harvest has an opportunity to shift the narrative from "what's missing" to "what we're building together." Grant applications can reference this media gap directly: the region lacks a positive, asset-based story.

## Curated Articles: Most Relevant to The Harvest

The following 18 articles were selected from the full corpus for their direct relevance to The Harvest's zones, programming, and strategic positioning.

### Local Economy & Markets

- **"Markets pump $54m into Coast economy each year"** -- Eumundi Markets economic impact study. Demonstrates the proven economic model for community gathering spaces. Direct precedent for The Harvest as an economic multiplier.
- **"Small business Saturday shines light on hinterland makers"** -- Profiles of artisan producers across Maleny, Montville, and surrounds. Validates the maker economy The Harvest aims to support.
- **"Hinterland tourism operators call for better signage and access"** -- Identifies infrastructure gap that community hubs can help address through wayfinding and visitor orientation.

### Arts & Culture

- **"'So loved': tributes for much-respected festival founder"** -- Woodford/Maleny Folk Festival legacy. Shows deep cultural roots and community investment in arts programming. The Harvest inherits this lineage.
- **"Artists without studios: the hinterland's hidden creative crisis"** -- Documents the lack of affordable creative workspace. Direct alignment with Art Space zone programming.
- **"Maleny music scene punches above its weight"** -- Celebrates existing cultural infrastructure while noting venue limitations. Opportunity for The Harvest as a performance and rehearsal space.
- **"Community theatre group searches for permanent home"** -- Another displaced creative community seeking space. Potential early tenant or programming partner.

### Food & Agriculture

- **"Farm-to-fork trail puts hinterland on the food map"** -- Existing food tourism infrastructure The Harvest can plug into. Kitchen zone narrative alignment.
- **"Young farmers find their feet on Sunshine Coast"** -- Next-generation agriculture stories. Garden zone programming relevance and potential collaborator profiles.
- **"Community garden feeds 40 families through winter"** -- Direct precedent for community food growing. Demonstrates demand and impact metrics useful for grant applications.

### Community Spirit

- **"Survey shows strong support for Dark Sky Reserve"** -- 95% support from 1,200 respondents. Proves the hinterland community will mobilise around shared environmental and cultural values. Model for The Harvest's own community consultation.
- **"Volunteers rebuild hall after storm damage"** -- Community resilience narrative. Shows the barn-raising spirit The Harvest can channel.
- **"Neighbourhood centres report record demand post-COVID"** -- Quantifies the need for community gathering infrastructure. Grant-relevant data point.

### Environment & Heritage

- **"Desperate race to resurrect 'zombie' tree in hinterland town"** -- Rhodamnia rubescens (scrub turpentine) threatened by myrtle rust. Links to biodiversity data in [[01-demographics]]. Garden zone education opportunity.
- **"Decades-long fire study shows benefit of planned burns"** -- Peachester State Forest research. Indigenous land management practices relevance. Potential programming alignment with Jinibara Country acknowledgement.

### Climate & Disaster

- **"Roads closed, multiple rescues in flash flooding"** -- Ex-Cyclone Alfred impact on the hinterland. Documents the vulnerability that makes community resilience infrastructure essential.
- **"Hinterland residents push for better emergency communications"** -- Community hub as emergency gathering point is a proven grant narrative.

### Housing

- **"90 of 95 Coast suburbs now median over $1M"** -- Housing affordability crisis quantified. Context for why community-accessible spaces matter -- people are spending more on housing and have less for everything else.

> [!insight] Food is consistently positive -- Kitchen zone narrative is strong
> Of 9 food and agriculture articles, the majority carry positive sentiment. Farm-to-table, community gardens, and young farmer stories all align directly with the Kitchen zone. This is a theme the media already wants to tell -- The Harvest just needs to be part of it.

> [!insight] Community spirit = 16 positive articles, proven engagement appetite
> The community spirit theme is the only major category that is entirely positive. Volunteerism, collective action, and mutual aid are already celebrated in local media. The Harvest's co-design ethos (kids build the kids area, artists shape the art space) fits a narrative the region already values.

## Narrative Opportunities for The Harvest

### 1. Reframe the Infrastructure Story

Infrastructure complaints dominate local media (34 articles). The Harvest can offer a counter-narrative: community-led infrastructure that people actually want. Grant language: "addressing the infrastructure deficit through community asset building."

### 2. Claim the Arts & Culture Space

With 24 articles documenting both a thriving creative community and a lack of permanent spaces, The Harvest's Art Space zone fills a documented gap. Multiple articles describe displaced creative groups actively seeking a home.

### 3. Lead with Food

Food and agriculture stories are consistently positive and well-covered. The Kitchen zone has the easiest media entry point. Farm-to-table programming, community meals, and food preservation workshops all have existing media precedent.

### 4. Build on Proven Community Engagement

The Dark Sky Reserve survey (1,200 respondents, 95% support) demonstrates that hinterland residents will engage with well-framed community initiatives. The Harvest's consultation and co-design process can reference this as a benchmark.

Are there community newsletters, Facebook groups, or hyperlocal channels that carry more weight than formal media in the hinterland?

## Methodology

Articles were sourced via web scraping and RSS monitoring of Sunshine Coast News and myPolice Sunshine Coast. Each article was coded for:

1. **Primary theme** -- the dominant topic (single code per article)
2. **Sentiment** -- positive, negative, mixed, or neutral based on tone and framing
3. **Relevance** -- scored 1-5 for alignment with The Harvest's mission and zones

Articles scoring 4-5 on relevance were included in the curated list above. The full corpus of 101 articles is available in the research data archive.
