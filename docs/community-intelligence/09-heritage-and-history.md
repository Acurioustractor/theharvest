Source: Queensland Heritage Register, Department of Environment, Science and Innovation. Five entries within the hinterland area surrounding Witta.

| # | Place | Address | Description |
|---|-------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Conondale Timbers Sawmill | Aherns Road, Conondale | Sawmill site with structures associated with sawmilling operations on an elevated portion above Elaman Creek. ~15km NW of Maleny. |
| 2 | St Isidore's | 40 Post Office Road, Mapleton | Large, high-set timber residence on the eastern edge of the Blackall Range with extensive views over Maroochy Plains to the Pacific Ocean. Timber-framed, weatherboard clad. |
| 3 | Kenilworth Homestead | Eumundi Kenilworth Road, Kenilworth | Large complex on ~38 hectares, accessed via Eumundi-Kenilworth Road, ~3km west of Kenilworth, on a bluff overlooking the Mary River. |
| 4 | Fairview | 15 Porter's Lane, Maleny | Low-set timber house on the steep slope forming the backdrop to Maleny township on the Blackall Range. Corrugated iron clad, short ridge roof with gablets. Single-skin, vertically-jointed timber boards, diamond braced. |
| 5 | Montville Memorial Precinct | Razorback Road and Main Street, Montville | Leafy area centred on Village Green, includes Memorial Close, memorial gates, memorial fig trees, Montville Hall, and Montville Memorial Hall plus St Marys Church Hall. |

> [!insight] Fairview is the closest QLD Heritage site
> Fairview (Maleny) is the only Queensland Heritage Register entry in the immediate Maleny-Witta area. Its vernacular timber construction -- corrugated iron, diamond bracing, single-skin boards -- is a tangible record of the hinterland's settler-era building traditions. The Conondale Timbers Sawmill connects directly to the timber industry that shaped the Range's early economy.

## First Nations Heritage

**Jinibara Country**

Witta and The Harvest sit on Jinibara land. The Jinibara people are the Traditional Owners of the Blackall Range and surrounding areas, with deep connections to the landscape stretching back tens of thousands of years. The broader Sunshine Coast region also holds significance for the Kabi Kabi (Gubbi Gubbi) people.

This knowledge base does not attempt to document or interpret First Nations cultural heritage -- that knowledge belongs to the Jinibara and Kabi Kabi peoples and should only be shared on their terms, in their voices.

**What we can note**:
- Any heritage, placemaking, or land-use work at The Harvest should be grounded in genuine relationship with Jinibara elders and community
- Truth-telling and acknowledgment are not boxes to tick but ongoing commitments
- First Nations cultural heritage is protected under the Aboriginal Cultural Heritage Act 2003 (Qld)

> [!question] Relationship building
> Partner with Jinibara elders for truth-telling and cultural guidance? How can The Harvest support Jinibara-led storytelling rather than speaking about First Nations heritage?

## Known Historical Context

Sources: Local knowledge, published histories, community memory. Not yet verified against archival records.

### Timber and Dairy

The Blackall Range was opened to European settlement in the 1870s-1880s, driven by the timber industry. Red cedar, hoop pine, and bunya pine were the primary draws. Once the big timber was cut, dairy farming became the dominant land use from the early 1900s through the mid-20th century. Witta was part of this dairy landscape. The Conondale Timbers Sawmill (QLD Heritage Register) is a surviving artefact of this era.

### Cooperative Tradition

Maleny has an unusually strong cooperative economy tradition that is itself a form of living heritage:
- **Maleny Credit Union** (now part of a larger credit union network) -- community-owned financial institution
- **Maple Street Co-op** -- long-running food cooperative on Maleny's main street
- **Up Front Club** -- community-owned arts and music venue

> [!insight] Maleny's cooperative tradition is living heritage worth documenting
> The cooperative economy of the Range -- credit union, food co-op, community arts venue -- is not listed on any heritage register but represents a distinctive and nationally significant community tradition. The Harvest sits within this lineage. Documenting it (oral histories, timeline, institutional records) would strengthen grant narratives and community identity.

### Cultural Landmarks

- **Maleny Folk Festival** (est. 1987, later became **Woodford Folk Festival**) -- founded by Bill Hauritz (d. 2025). One of Australia's most significant community cultural events, born on the Range before relocating to Woodford.
- **Crystal Waters Eco Village** (est. 1987) -- one of Australia's first permaculture villages, located near Conondale. A pioneer of intentional community design and sustainable land use.

> [!question] Oral history urgency
> Document oral histories before they're lost? The generation that built the cooperative economy, established Crystal Waters, and founded the Folk Festival is aging. Recording their stories is time-sensitive heritage work.
