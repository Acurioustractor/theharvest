## DIRECT Relevance Consultations

These five consultations intersect most strongly with The Harvest's location, mission, or programming.

### 4. Hinterland Futures -- Planning Scheme Review

- **Status**: Closed
- **Relevance**: DIRECT -- planning scheme amendments affecting rural/hinterland zones including Maleny and Witta
- **Key Themes**: rural_character, development_pressure, agricultural_land, housing_density, infrastructure
- **Harvest Alignment**: The Harvest operates within a rural zone. Understanding planning scheme constraints and opportunities is essential for any built-form development, change-of-use applications, or infrastructure proposals on the site. The review's emphasis on preserving rural character aligns with the Harvest ethos of working with the land rather than against it.

### 6. Maleny Precinct Plan

- **Status**: Closed
- **Relevance**: DIRECT -- Maleny is the nearest town centre and primary service hub for Witta
- **Key Themes**: town_character, parking, pedestrian_access, retail_mix, heritage_buildings, tourism_management
- **Harvest Alignment**: The Harvest draws visitors through Maleny. Understanding parking constraints, tourism management priorities, and town-character protections helps position the Harvest as a complementary destination that disperses visitor pressure rather than adding to it. The Kitchen zone's focus on local produce strengthens Maleny's existing food-culture identity.

### 9. Disaster Management -- Community Resilience

- **Status**: Closed
- **Relevance**: DIRECT -- bushfire, flood, and storm preparedness in rural hinterland
- **Key Themes**: bushfire_preparedness, flood_resilience, community_networks, rural_isolation, emergency_access
- **Harvest Alignment**: Rural hinterland properties face real disaster risk. The Harvest as a community gathering space can serve as a social resilience node -- a place where neighbours already know each other, share resources, and coordinate response. This is a strong grant narrative: community hubs reduce isolation and build the informal networks that activate during emergencies.

### 10. Reconciliation Action Plan

- **Status**: Closed
- **Relevance**: DIRECT -- engagement with Jinibara and Kabi Kabi peoples on Jinibara Country
- **Key Themes**: reconciliation, first_nations_engagement, cultural_heritage, truth_telling, partnerships
- **Harvest Alignment**: The Harvest sits on Jinibara Country. The co-design ethos -- where community members shape the spaces they use -- extends naturally to First Nations engagement. Any programming involving land, food, or art has an opportunity (and responsibility) to centre indigenous knowledge and practice. RAP alignment strengthens grant applications and demonstrates genuine commitment beyond compliance.

### 11. Neighbourhood and Targeted Community Projects

- **Status**: **OPEN**
- **Relevance**: DIRECT -- ongoing consultation on neighbourhood-scale community projects
- **Key Themes**: neighbourhood_projects, local_identity, community_connection, placemaking, participation
- **Harvest Alignment**: This is the most actionable consultation. The Harvest fits squarely within the scope of neighbourhood-level placemaking and community connection. Participation in this consultation signals engagement with council processes and creates a paper trail of alignment that strengthens future grant applications.

> [!insight] 4 consultations have DIRECT relevance to Witta/Maleny
> Hinterland Futures, Maleny Precinct Plan, Disaster Management, and the Reconciliation Action Plan all directly intersect with The Harvest's location, mission, or community obligations. A fifth -- Neighbourhood Projects -- is both DIRECT and currently OPEN.

> [!insight] Neighbourhood Projects consultation is still OPEN -- submit now
> This is the only active consultation with direct relevance. A submission from The Harvest would establish council-level engagement and create a documented touchpoint for future grant narratives. Even a brief expression of interest positions the project within SCC's community development pipeline.

## Regional Relevance Consultations

These consultations operate at the broader Sunshine Coast level but contain themes, funding mechanisms, or policy frameworks relevant to The Harvest.

### 1. Sunshine Coast Community Strategy 2019-2041

Long-term strategy for community wellbeing, social infrastructure, and community development across the region. The strategy explicitly calls for more community gathering spaces -- The Harvest fills this gap.

### 2. Environment and Liveability Strategy 2017

Environmental sustainability, biodiversity corridors, and liveable communities. The Garden zone aligns with council's biodiversity corridor and green infrastructure priorities. Native plantings, habitat restoration, and food forests all speak this strategy's language.

### 3. Arts and Heritage Levy

Arts and Heritage Levy funding priorities for the Sunshine Coast region.

> [!insight] Arts and Heritage Levy = most actionable funding pathway
> The levy creates a direct, recurring funding mechanism for arts and cultural infrastructure. The Harvest's Art Space zone is purpose-built for this alignment. Programs involving local artists, heritage skills, and creative workshops map directly onto levy funding criteria.

### 5. Sunshine Coast Mass Transit Options

Mass transit planning with hinterland connectivity as a recurring theme. Transport access affects who can reach The Harvest -- particularly relevant for equity and accessibility arguments in grant applications.

### 7. Community Grants Program Review

Review of SCC's community grants framework. Understanding how council evaluates grant applications informs how The Harvest frames its own submissions. Key criteria typically include community benefit, sustainability, and volunteer/community capacity.

### 8. Sunshine Coast Recreation Parks and Open Space Plan

Recreation spaces, parks, trails, and community gathering areas. The Harvest's outdoor spaces -- garden, gathering areas, nature play -- align with this plan's priorities around accessible recreation and community gathering in natural settings.

### 12. Coastal Resilience and Climate Adaptation

Climate impacts and natural hazards across the region. While coastal-focused, the adaptation frameworks and resilience thinking apply to hinterland contexts. Useful framing for disaster-preparedness grant narratives.

### 13. Coastal Resilience Projects

Community input on foreshore projects at Golden Beach, Bulcock Beach, and Diamond Head. Status unknown. Limited direct relevance but demonstrates SCC's pattern of community consultation on infrastructure projects.

## Planning Zones -- Witta Area

Source: SCC ArcGIS planning data. **200 features** mapped in the Witta area.

**Dominant zones:**

- **Environmental Management and Conservation Zone** -- largest area coverage. Reflects the biodiversity corridor network through the hinterland. Significant constraints on development but strong alignment with Garden zone conservation and restoration activities.
- **Open Space Zone** -- recreation, community gathering, and green space. Supports outdoor programming and nature-based activities.
- **Rural zones** -- agricultural and rural-residential land. The Harvest likely sits within or adjacent to rural-zoned land. Key considerations: permissible uses, material change of use triggers, building height and setback requirements, agricultural land protection provisions.

> What is the exact zoning of The Harvest property? What uses are permissible as-of-right versus requiring development approval? Are there any overlays (flood, bushfire, heritage, biodiversity) that affect the site? SCC's PD Online mapping tool can answer these questions precisely.

> [!insight] RAP alignment strengthens grant applications involving First Nations engagement

This is not just compliance -- it signals to grant assessors that the project operates within an established reconciliation context. Co-design with Jinibara community members adds authenticity and impact.
