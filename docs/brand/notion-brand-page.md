## Supporting Story Threads

Timber, dairy, milk crates, shared tables, shop tests, art, and co-operative practice support the brand. They are not the top-line public spine.

## Launch frame

Current frame: **garden opening end of June**.

Use the exact operational date only where it is confirmed and needed. The current brand line is softer and simpler.

Operational gates still need checking before public promises go out:

- public liability insurance
- food safety
- pizza lead
- extra hands and roster
- parking
- source, rights, and consent for public media

## Voice

Write like someone standing at the gate.

Use:

- plain sentences
- named rooms
- named objects
- local detail
- practical asks
- one clear invitation

Avoid:

- "we are excited to announce"
- "vibrant community hub"
- polished venue language
- startup gradients
- big claims before the place can hold them

## Visual cues

Use:

- milk crates
- reclaimed timber
- rammed earth
- rusted iron
- garden beds
- floor plan marks
- long tables
- real site photos
- historical Witta and Teutoburg photos
- scans, books, archive notes, and local story fragments
- hands, tools, pots, plates, paths

Avoid:

- AI-generated brand imagery
- generic stock imagery
- abstract gradients
- decorative blobs
- over-polished hospitality imagery
- anything that hides the work underway

## Real source rule

The Harvest brand should look like the place, the people, and the history.

AI can help arrange a slide, crop a photo, draft a caption, or build a layout from supplied assets. It should not invent fake Harvest scenes, fake people, fake interiors, or fake history.

Every public image needs:

- source
- rights or permission
- consent status, if people are visible
- attribution
- story note
