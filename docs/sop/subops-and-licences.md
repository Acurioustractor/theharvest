# Sub-operators and licence holders: someone else runs a thing on our land

**Who does this:** a steward (Susie or Joey) at the front, with Ben or Nic doing any sign-off. You do the intake and the day-to-day. You never sign an agreement yourself.

**The screen(s) you need:** GHL/LeadConnector (the relationship and the notes), Square POS (only if their goods go on the shelf and get sold through our till), and Paper (the maker pack folder, the consent tick, the come-back sheet). Events go through Humanitix. Crew chatter goes through WhatsApp. Nothing else.

First, know which kind of "someone else" you have. There are two, and they are not the same.

- **A shelf maker (a producer).** They put produce or made goods on our shared shelf. The maker keeps ownership of the item until it sells. Only when it sells do they get paid the agreed split: makers keep about 75 to 80 percent, the shop keeps 20 to 25 percent. This is consignment. It is low risk for the maker. This is happening now.
- **A sub-operator on the future commercial layer (the cafe or retail, sublicensed).** This is the future commercial phase, not the community shelf. "Retail sublicensed" means that future layer. If someone asks to run a commercial operation on the land, that is not a steward decision. Send it to Ben or Nic. ## Steps for a shelf maker (the path you will actually run)

1. They show interest through the `/shop` form, or they ask you in person (the Knock). If it was in person, write it on the come-back sheet so the Monday sweep types it in.
2. In GHL, their card sits in the Shop pipeline at **New interest**. Do not blast them. Book a real chat through the "book a chat about the shop" calendar.
3. When they reply or you have the chat, drag the card from **New interest** to **In conversation**.
4. Hand them the **maker pack** (one kraft folder, paper first):
   - The consignment agreement (one page: the split and the payout day). [DECIDE: the rest of the one-page agreement terms, such as insurance and how either side ends it, since the source docs only lock the split and payout, not the contract wording]
   - Their story card (who they are, what they make, where) with the photo consent tick-box.
   - The shelf label template (their name and origin; the pledge does the price talking).
   - A food tier check if it is food (see the food box below).
5. Get the consent tick on the story card. Note it in the GHL contact. No tick, no photo of them used, ever.
6. When their first goods come in to try, move the card to **Sampling / trial shelf**. Set them up in Square, one item per maker product, with the maker's name in the item so the sales report can be filtered. Once they are a live stockist, move the card to **On the shelf**.
7. If it sold, tell the maker that day. A one-line WhatsApp with a photo of the first shelf placement. This is your job, not the software's.

## The food rule for a maker (Phase A, council to confirm)

- Fresh whole fruit and vegetables: fine to sell on the shelf.
- Pre-packaged shelf-stable food (preserves, jam, honey, dried, baked, ferments): fine **only if the maker holds their own Queensland home-based food business licence.** The maker carries the food-safety duty under their own licence. The shop is just the retail outlet.
- High-risk perishables (chilled dairy, meat, ready-to-eat food needing temperature control): **not yet.** Do not put these on the shelf.
- The shop itself does not hold a food business licence for the shelf. This must be confirmed in writing with the Sunshine Coast Council Environmental Health team before the first sale.

## The signage rule

- The shop-wide pledge board says makers keep about [DECIDE: the exact percent within 75 to 80, confirmed by Standard Ledger before the board is printed]. Hold the printed percent until that is confirmed.
- Each product names its maker and origin.
- Never put any one maker's own pricing or margin on display. The pledge is shop-wide; the individual numbers stay private.

## Never do (read this twice)

- Never sign a consignment agreement or a sublicence yourself. Ben or Nic signs.
- Never put a maker's goods on the shelf before the consent tick and (for food) the licence check.
- Never put high-risk perishables on the shelf.
- Never display an individual maker's pricing or margin.
- Never blast makers as a generic list. Maker outreach is one to one, in a set order, from a person.
- Never pay a maker before the item sells. Consignment means paid on sale only.
- Never move a maker's card up just because they RSVP'd to something. Cards move on a real action.
- Never use GHL to sell or to pay a maker. Square is the till and the payouts. GHL is only the relationship record.

## When stuck

Ask Ben or Nic. Anything involving a signature, a commercial sub-operator on the land, a council/licence question, or money you are unsure about goes to them, same day. The Monday sweep is where loose ends get caught, so if in doubt, write it on the paper pile and flag it.
