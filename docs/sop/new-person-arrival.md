# Someone new walks in: sorting the lanes with warmth

> A first-week steward can follow this. Short steps. Plain words. When a detail
> is not decided yet, it says so. Do not guess one in.

## Who does this

Susie, Joey, or any steward or volunteer on the floor when a new person turns up
or gets in touch. Ben and Nic sit behind you for the hand-read calls.

## The screen(s) you need

GHL (LeadConnector app) for the relationship. Paper (the come-back sheet on the
table) for catching it in the moment. That is all you touch for a first hello.
Square, Humanitix, and WhatsApp come later, only once you know which lane the
person is on.

## The four lanes (read this once)

Sort people by their energy, not by money. A person can be on more than one lane
at the same time. The four lanes are:

1. **Public.** Came to eat, gather, belong. Most people stay here, and that is
   success.
2. **Makers / users.** Came to build or use the place: the garden, the art space,
   work days, the kids' corner.
3. **Producers.** Came to put something on the shop shelf to sell.
4. **Community.** Jinibara, elders, storytellers. A different grammar. Worked by
   a human, by hand, consent first, never a workflow.

## Steps

1. **Say hello like a neighbour.** Plain and warm. No pitch. You are meeting a
   person, not capturing a lead.

2. **Listen for the energy. Ask yourself which lane.**
   - "I just came for the afternoon" or they ate and left happy -> **Public.**
   - "Can I help build that?" or they lingered and offered to come back ->
     **Maker / user.**
   - "I'd put my eggs (or jam, or mugs) on your shelf" -> **Producer.**
   - A Jinibara elder, or someone sharing a story -> **Community.**

3. **Catch it on paper.** Put their name and what they said on the come-back
   sheet on the table. A phone call or a note on a kraft label counts just as
   much as a form. The Monday sweep types the paper in. Barry never needs an app.

4. **Now follow the lane.**

   **If Public:** invite them to follow along by choice (the footer follow form
   on the website). That is it. Leave them be. Do not put them in a pipeline. Do
   not drip them. A tap or an RSVP is not a lead.

   **If Maker / user:** invite them to a work day or a crew. The ask is for a
   pair of hands, not just attention: "we are building the kids' corner, who is
   in?" They climb the rungs (curious, connected, member, active, steward) only
   by DOING, and only a human hand-moves a rung in the Monday sweep. Never enrol
   them from an RSVP click.

   **If Producer:** this is the Shop pipeline (a separate commercial track). In
   GHL, make sure they land at "New interest" and book them a chat about the
   shop. Tell them the honest offer: makers keep about 75 to 80 percent, the shop
   keeps 20 to 25 percent to cover card fees and overheads, and we name you on
   the shelf. The maker keeps ownership of the item until it sells. Only on sale
   are they paid the agreed split. Low risk for them. (Square runs the till and
   the payouts, not GHL. You do not need to set that up at hello.)

   **If Community:** do nothing automatic. No form, no pipeline, no list, no tag
   beyond `lane:community`. Tell Ben or Nic. Any name, story, or photo gets a
   consent check first, every time it is used, and consent can be pulled back
   any time. Elder authority is a veto, not a preference.

5. **Close the loop like a person.** If they helped on a day, a one-line personal
   thank-you beats any dashboard. If a maker's first item sells, tell them that
   day: a one-line WhatsApp with a photo of their first shelf placement. That is
   staff work, not software.

6. **Leave it for the Monday sweep to sort.** You do not have to file everything
   now. The sweep (30 minutes, one person) reads the paper pile, moves rungs by
   hand, and reads three numbers out loud. The sweep is the heartbeat. If it gets
   skipped two weeks running, the system is quietly broken.

## Never do

- Never auto-climb a public person into a pipeline, drip them, or read a tap or
  an RSVP as a lead.
- Never charge anyone for a rung, and never move someone up a rung just because
  they showed up. Rungs are earned by a real action and hand-moved on Monday.
- Never blast producers as a generic shop list. Maker outreach is one to one
  (email, DM, or call) in the set order.
- Never expose any single maker's own price or margin. The signage is a shop-wide
  pledge ("makers keep about X percent") plus each product naming its maker and
  origin.
- Never put a community-lane person (Jinibara, elder, storyteller) into any
  workflow, list, or drip. By hand, with consent, always. Use a community name,
  story, or photo only after a consent check, and only while consent still holds.
- Never add a tag while sending a broadcast (adding a tag is what fires a welcome
  email, so a tagging broadcast can spam a whole list).
- Never send to "All". A broadcast goes to one named list, and you read the
  contact count first.
- Never open Marketing, Automation, or Settings in GHL. Stewards stay in
  Conversations, Opportunities, Calendars, and Contacts.
- Never invent a count, a date, a name, a price, or a photo consent. In a town of
  about 1,296 people, a wrong figure or an uncleared photo costs trust you cannot
  rebuild. When the true thing is not ready, leave the blank.
- Voice gate before anything you write: no em-dashes; never use vibrant, tapestry,
  testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, seamless, or delve; say "work days"
  not "working bees"; place names are Witta on Jinibara Country (colonial names
  in brackets).

## When stuck

Ask Ben or Nic. Anything about a community-lane person (Jinibara, elder,
storyteller), any consent question, or any money question goes to them before you
act.

## A note on tools

There are exactly four screens plus paper: GHL (relationships), Square (the
till), Humanitix (events), WhatsApp (crews), and Paper (the room). Nothing else
gets added. There is no community app or login to learn. Three tills, never
confused: things on shelves go through Square, tickets go through Humanitix,
belonging and membership go through a GHL link. You only ever need to know which
till a thing goes through.
