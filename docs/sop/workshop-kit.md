# The Harvest workshop kit: a half-day to learn the place

A plan for one half-day session to train Susie, Joey, Nic and any volunteers on running The Harvest with clarity and flow. The whole point is practice, not slides. People learn by doing the thing once in a safe room, with the real screens and the real paper in front of them.

Print this page. Bring the paper packs. Have the four screens logged in on a phone or two before you start.

## Welcome and the one idea (15 min)

1. Sit everyone in a circle. No screens yet.
2. Say the one idea out loud: **four screens plus paper, the rest is warmth.**
3. Name the four screens, slowly, one at a time:
   - **GHL** holds the relationships (people, notes, the pipelines).
   - **Square** is the till for the shop shelf.
   - **Humanitix** runs events and tickets.
   - **WhatsApp** carries the crews.
   - **Paper** is the room (the come-back sheet, the labels, the rosters on the wall).
4. Say the rule that goes with it: you only ever need to know which screen a thing goes through. Pick the screen by what the thing is.
5. Say the other half out loud too: the warmth is the job. The screens just keep the promise. A one-line personal thank-you beats any dashboard.
6. Tell them how the day works: five short drills, one for each big job, then a talk at the end about the things still to decide.

## Drill 1: Onboarding a maker with the paper pack (15 min)

**Teaches:** shop-maker-onboarding.md. **Screen used:** GHL plus Paper (the kraft maker folder).

1. Pair people up. One plays the maker (say, someone with a jar of jam). One plays the steward.
2. The steward makes up a kraft maker folder by hand. Put four things in it:
   - The consignment agreement (one page: the split, the payout day, who insures what, and the line that either side can end it with a conversation).
   - The story card (who they are, what they make, where, plus the photo-consent tick box).
   - The shelf label template.
   - A food tier check if the item is food.
3. Walk through the story card together. The maker ticks whether their photo and story can be used. If they do not tick it, no photo or story goes on a label or online. Full stop.
4. Note that consent in the maker's GHL contact, and keep the signed card in the folder.
5. If the item is food, check the tier out loud: fresh whole fruit and veg are fine; pre-packaged shelf-stable food needs the maker to hold their own Queensland home-based food business licence. No chilled dairy, meat, or ready-to-eat food at launch.
6. Practise the honest offer in plain words: makers keep about 75 to 80 percent, the shop keeps 20 to 25 percent to cover card fees and overhead, and we name you on the shelf. The maker keeps ownership of the item until it sells.
7. Swap roles and run it again with a different pretend maker.

## Drill 2: A mock Monday sweep on the real boards (15 min)

**Teaches:** monday-sweep.md. **Screen used:** GHL plus Paper (the come-back sheets).

1. One person runs the sweep. The others watch and call out anything missed.
2. Open the Harvest Inbox pipeline. Look at the "New" column. For each card, either answer it now or give it a next-action date. Nothing in "New" is left with no plan.
3. Open Conversations. Reply to anything unread. Nothing sits unread for more than 3 days.
4. Open the Shop board. Any card without a next-action date gets one.
5. Take a pretend paper pile (a few come-back sheets you write out before the session) and type them in. Use tags from the tag map only. Never make up a new tag on the spot.
6. Practise the hardest rule out loud: only move someone up a rung if they DID something real this week (helped, came back, brought eggs). An RSVP or a turning-up is not a rung.
7. Read three numbers out loud: members, RSVPs for the next event, and makers on the shelf. If a number surprises you, find out why before doing anything else.
8. Remind everyone why this drill matters: the sweep is the heartbeat. If it gets skipped two weeks running, the system is quietly broken.

## Drill 3: Duplicate a Humanitix event and change the date (15 min)

**Teaches:** gatherings-open-days.md. **Screen used:** Humanitix.

1. Open the Humanitix organiser app. Do not build an event from scratch.
2. Open the "Work Day" or "Workshop" template, duplicate it, and change the date. That is the whole job.
3. Set a seat cap if the room is tight. [DECIDE: confirm the seat cap for each space.] If the cap is not set yet, leave it blank and ask, do not guess a number in.
4. Add a waitlist if it might fill.
5. Practise the invite order out loud, because it never changes: crew WhatsApp groups first, then members by email, then the public last, if at all. The people building the place hear first.
6. Write a pretend invite as an ask for hands, not attendance. "We're building the kids' corner, who's in?" beats "come along".
7. Say the rule about reminders: Humanitix sends the reminder and the day-before nudge by itself. You do not send those by hand. Do not add a tag to anyone as part of inviting them.

## Drill 4: A pretend sale and the payout conversation (15 min)

**Teaches:** money-handling.md (with shop-maker-onboarding.md). **Screen used:** Square.

1. Set up a pretend item on the shelf with a maker's name on it.
2. One person plays the customer. One plays the steward.
3. Ring the item up in Square POS. Each item is listed under its maker, so the sale records against the right person.
4. Take a pretend payment on the Square Reader (tap or chip), hand over the item, the receipt is automatic.
5. Say the rule out loud: you do not work out the split at the till. Square records the sale. The payout happens later.
6. Now role-play the payout conversation a maker might start. Practise these honest lines:
   - "You keep about 75 to 80 percent, the shop keeps 20 to 25 percent for card fees and overhead." [DECIDE: the exact split inside that band, confirmed by Standard Ledger before the pledge percent is printed.] Until that is confirmed, do not say or print a fixed percent.
   - "Each sale is recorded against you in Square, so the split is clean and I can show it."
   - "Payouts run by bank transfer on a set rhythm." [DECIDE: the payout day and rhythm, weekly or monthly, confirmed with Standard Ledger.] If a maker asks for the exact day and it is not set, say you will confirm it, do not invent one.
7. Practise the never-rules: never sell or pay a maker through GHL (Square is the till), and never tell a maker or write on a sign what any single maker is paid in dollars.

## Drill 5: Sort three pretend arrivals into lanes (15 min)

**Teaches:** new-person-arrival.md. **Screen used:** GHL plus Paper (the come-back sheet).

1. Write three short cards before the session, one pretend new person on each. For example: someone who just came for the afternoon; someone who offered to help build the kids' corner; someone who wants to put their eggs on the shelf.
2. Read the four lanes out loud first: Public, Maker/user, Producer, Community.
3. Hand the three cards out. Each person says which lane the arrival is on, and why, from the energy not the money.
4. Then say what happens next on that lane:
   - **Public:** invite them to follow along by choice. Leave them be. Do not put them in a pipeline.
   - **Maker / user:** invite them to a work day or a crew. Ask for a pair of hands. They climb rungs only by doing, hand-moved on Monday.
   - **Producer:** land them in the Shop pipeline at "New interest" and book a chat. Tell them the honest offer.
   - **Community:** do nothing automatic. No form, no pipeline, no list. Tell Ben or Nic. Any name, story, or photo gets a consent check first, every time.
5. Catch each one on the paper come-back sheet, and say the line: leave it for the Monday sweep to sort. You do not have to file everything now.
6. Close the drill with the hardest never-rule, said plainly: never put a community-lane person (Jinibara, elder, storyteller) into any workflow, list, or drip. By hand, with consent, always. Elder authority is a veto, not a preference.

## When it breaks: who to ask (15 min)

Go round the group and read these out, one job at a time. The pattern is simple: when stuck, you ask a person. You never guess.

1. **New person, lane, consent, or money question:** ask Ben or Nic before you act (new-person-arrival.md).
2. **The Monday sweep:** ask Ben. Anything to do with elders, storytellers, or the community lane, ask Ben or Nic before you do a single thing (monday-sweep.md).
3. **A maker, the split, or the Square setup:** ask Ben or Nic. The split wording and the Square setup are theirs to confirm with Standard Ledger (shop-maker-onboarding.md).
4. **A split, a payout, or which entity money belongs to:** ask Susie or Joey first; if it is about a split or payout, that goes to Ben or Nic. When in doubt, take nothing and write it on the come-back sheet for the Monday sweep (money-handling.md).
5. **A gathering, the broadcasts, or the gate:** ask Ben (broadcasts, workflows, Humanitix setup) or Nic (the day, the gate, the crews) (gatherings-open-days.md).
6. **A work day or the room:** ask Nic about the room and the day; ask Ben about the event build, any email, or anything to do with tags, workflows, elders or storytellers (build-days-and-volunteers.md).
7. **Anything touching an elder, a storyteller, or Jinibara:** ask Ben or Nic first, always, before you act. This one never changes.

Say the rule under all of them: when the true thing is not ready, leave the blank and ask. In a small town a wrong figure or an uncleared photo costs trust you cannot rebuild.

## The open decisions: a group conversation (close)

These are the things the SOPs cannot answer yet. They are not a steward's to guess. Read them out and talk through who owns each one and when it gets decided. This is the honest end of the day: the place is real, and some of it is still being figured out together.

### The shop and the money
- [DECIDE: the exact split inside 75 to 80 percent, confirmed by Standard Ledger, before the shop-wide pledge percent is printed.]
- [DECIDE: the payout day and rhythm, weekly or monthly, and whether Square lands under the right entity given the 30 June cutover, confirmed with Standard Ledger.]
- [DECIDE: confirm the phase-one food position in writing with the Sunshine Coast Council Environmental Health team before the first food sale. This is a single phone call, owned by Ben or Nic.]
- [DECIDE: the exact open-hour days and times for phase one. The docs lock "weekend or market-day windows" but do not name the hours.]
- [DECIDE: the fixed weekly drop-off window for makers (a habit, not a booking system), printed on the wall once Susie/Joey and Nic set it.]

### Membership
- [DECIDE: the membership price and when paid membership starts, decided after 20 June with real members in the room. Only charge once the value is obvious, and a human confirms the relationship before any charge.]

### Events and crews
- [DECIDE: confirm the seat cap for each space.]
- [DECIDE: which crew WhatsApp groups exist and who the admin is for each (named in Phase 3, July, after the 20 June day).]
- [DECIDE: the Humanitix-to-GHL attendee path (native integration vs Zapier vs CSV). The fallback is a weekly CSV import on the Monday sweep. Confirm before the first July work day.]

### Stories, consent and heritage (community lane, Ben or Nic only)
- [DECIDE: the exact wording and tick-boxes on the oral-history consent form, and who drafts and approves it.]
- [DECIDE: where the audio files, photos, and transcripts get stored, who holds them long-term, and the folder convention.]
- [DECIDE: what owes-ledger entry we make for a captured story, and where it lives.]
- [DECIDE: where and how an event guest's photo consent is recorded, since the story-card tick only covers makers.]

### Bigger asks (Ben or Nic, with sign-off)
- [DECIDE: venue hire / team-day price and what is included.]
- [DECIDE: the steward-facing intake steps for a future commercial sub-operator.]
- [DECIDE: the rest of the one-page consignment agreement terms, such as insurance and how either side ends it.]
