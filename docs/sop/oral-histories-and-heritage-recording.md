# Oral histories: recording and archiving the Witta heritage

**Who does this:** Ben or Nic, by hand, every time. Never a steward on their own, never a volunteer, never an automation. This is community-lane work, and the community lane is always worked by a person.

**The screen(s) you need:** Paper (the consent form and your notebook, this is the main one), GHL (LeadConnector, to keep the relationship note), and WhatsApp only to arrange a time with a crew. Not Square. Not Humanitix. Nothing automated touches this work.

This is about recording stories, yarns, and heritage with people on Jinibara Country, around Witta (Maleny). Elders and storytellers hold their own stories. We are guests. Our job is to ask well, get a clear yes first, and look after what we are given.

## Before you start

1. Talk to Ben or Nic first. Always. If you are reading this and you are not Ben or Nic, stop here and ask one of them.
2. Make sure consent comes first. Nobody is recorded, photographed, or written down until they have said yes to being recorded, and yes to how it will be used. The yes comes before the record, never after.
3. Remember elder authority is a veto, not a preference. If an elder says no, or says not that part, that is the end of it. You do not negotiate it.
4. Bring paper. A consent form and a notebook. Paper is a real input here, the same as anywhere else at The Harvest.

## On the day (the steps)

1. Arrive as a guest. Use the place names right: Witta on Jinibara Country, the colonial name in brackets only if it helps. Never map or speak for Country without the authority to.
2. Explain plainly what you are doing and why, before any recording starts. What you would like to record. Where it might be used. That they can say no to any part.
3. Ask for consent out loud and on paper. Walk through the consent form together. Tick what they agree to (recording, a photo, using the story, naming them). Leave blank anything they do not agree to. [DECIDE: what is the exact wording and tick-boxes on the consent form, and who drafts and approves it]
4. Tell them they can change their mind at any time, and that we will honour it. Consent here is revocable, always.
5. Record the yarn. Let them lead. You are there to listen, not to steer.
6. Photos only if they ticked yes to a photo. No photo without a clear yes. If in doubt, leave the blank, do not take it.
7. Thank them in person, as a person, not as a step in a process.

## After the day

1. Save the recording and any photos to the agreed place. [DECIDE: where do the audio files, photos, and transcripts get stored, who holds them long-term, and what folder convention do we use]
2. Write a relationship note in GHL on their contact. Note that you met, what was shared, and exactly what they consented to. Tag the contact `lane:community`, plus `role:elder` or `role:storyteller` where that is true, plus a `consent:` note where you have one. Never put a `tier:` tag on a community contact. Never put them in a pipeline. Never add them to a drip or a list.
3. The story they gave is a gift, and we owe back. The yarn, photo, or transcript now sits on the owes-ledger: it means we have promised to use it well, under consent, and to bring it to life rather than leave it raw or use it without the conversation. [DECIDE: what is the owes-ledger entry we make for a captured story, and where does it live]
4. If we ever want to publish a name, story, or photo later, check consent again first, at the time of use. A yes once is not a yes forever. If you cannot confirm the consent, do not publish. Leave the blank.

## Never do (read this box twice)

- Never record, photograph, or write down a person before they have said yes.
- Never use "Aboriginal" as a catch-all. These are Jinibara people, named correctly.
- Never put a community contact in a pipeline, a drip, a workflow, or any automation. By hand, always.
- Never add a `tier:` tag to a community contact.
- Never publish a name, story, or photo without a fresh consent check at the time of use.
- Never invent a count, a date, a name, or a detail. If the true thing is not ready, leave the blank.
- Never use an em-dash. Never use the words vibrant, tapestry, testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, seamless, delve. Say "work days", not "working bees".
- Never overrule an elder. Their authority is a veto.

## When stuck

Ask Ben or Nic, before you do anything. Always. This is the one kind of work where you check first and act second.
