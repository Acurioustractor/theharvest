# Shop: getting a maker onto the shelf

**Who does this:** Susie or Joey (the stewards). Ben or Nic helps with the consignment split wording and the Square setup.

**The screen(s) you need:** GHL / LeadConnector app (the maker relationship), Square POS (the till, the catalogue, payouts), and Paper (the kraft maker folder, the shelf label, the roster on the wall). You will not need Humanitix or WhatsApp to put someone on the shelf, though WhatsApp is how you tell a maker their thing sold.

This is for makers and growers around Witta (on Jinibara Country) and Maleny who want to sell on the shelf on consignment. Consignment means the maker keeps ownership of their item until it sells. Only when it sells does the maker get paid their share. It is low risk for them. They keep about 75 to 80 percent of what the customer pays. The shop keeps 20 to 25 percent, which covers card fees, staffing, and overhead. ## The steps

1. **They land in the Shop pipeline.** Most makers come in through the `/shop` form on the website, which puts them in the Shop pipeline at the **New interest** stage and sends them an automatic receipt. Some come in from a 1:1 ask (the Knock). If you met someone in person who wants to sell, add them to the Shop pipeline by hand. Never broadcast to makers as a generic list. Outreach is 1:1, in a defined order, email or DM or call.

2. **Book a chat and have a real conversation.** Use the "Book a chat about the shop" calendar. When they reply or book, drag their card from **New interest** to **In conversation** in GHL. Talk about what they make, volumes, timing, and food safety where it is food.

3. **Make up their kraft maker folder (paper first).** One folder per maker, with four things:
   - The **consignment agreement**, one page: the split, the payout day, who insures what, and the line that either side can end it with a conversation.
   - Their **story card**: who they are, what they make, where, plus a photo-consent tick box. This feeds the honest signage and the consent note.
   - The **shelf label template** (step 7).
   - A **food tier check** where the item is food (step 5).

4. **Tick the consent box, and write it down.** On the story card, the maker ticks whether their photo and story can be used. Note that consent in their GHL contact and keep the signed card in their maker folder. No photo or story goes on a label or online without that tick.

5. **Check the food tier if it is food.** At launch the shelf only sells:
   - **Fresh whole fruit and vegetables.** These are fine to sell freely.
   - **Pre-packaged shelf-stable food** (preserves, jam, honey, dried, baked, ferments) from a maker who **holds their own Queensland home-based food business licence**. The maker carries the food-safety obligation under their own licence. The shop is just the retail outlet.
   No chilled dairy, meat, or ready-to-eat food that needs temperature control. Those are not on the shelf yet. The shop does not hold a shop-level food licence. [DECIDE: confirm this food position in writing with the Sunshine Coast Council Environmental Health team before the first sale.]

6. **Add their items to Square.** One item per maker product, or one item per maker with sub-SKUs. Put the maker's name in the item description or as an SKU prefix (for example the first three letters of the maker's name), so the weekly sales report can be filtered by maker. Square is the till. It runs sales, inventory, and the maker payouts. GHL never sells and never pays a maker.

7. **Make the shelf label.** Use the template. Each label names the maker and where the item is from. The maker confirms their own line so the story is theirs.
   ```
   [ITEM NAME] · [MAKER FIRST NAME] · [PROPERTY OR BUSINESS NAME]
   Made at [PLACE], from [SOURCE].
   $[PRICE]
   ```
   The shop-wide pledge board ("makers keep about X percent") does the talking about pay. A label never shows one maker's own pricing or margin.

8. **Move them to the shelf.** Once they have brought items in, drag the card to **Sampling / trial shelf**, then to **On the shelf** when they are a live stockist. Take a photo of their first shelf placement.

9. **Tell them it sold, the same day.** When a maker's item sells, send them a one-line WhatsApp with a photo of the shelf placement. This is steward work, not software. That photo is what turns a one-off maker into a regular.

10. **Pay them at the agreed split.** Square runs the payout. Each sale is recorded against the maker, so the split is clean and you can show it. Pay out weekly or monthly. At launch we reconcile by hand: export the Square sales report, split by maker, pay by bank transfer. [DECIDE: the payout day and rhythm, and whether Square lands under the right entity given the 30 June cutover, confirmed with Standard Ledger.]

11. **Restock and keep the relationship.** Keep the maker in GHL as the relationship record: offer tags, chat notes, the consent note, and the next action. The Monday sweep catches any card that has gone quiet.

## Hours and shifts

Open hours start small. Weekend or market-day windows only to begin, extended only as the shelf and foot traffic prove out. Stewards (Susie and Joey) anchor the open hours. Makers can do an occasional shift in exchange for shelf space, which also builds the community. The shift roster is a printed sheet on the shop wall plus a photo of it in the makers' WhatsApp group. No roster software unless paper breaks.

## Never do

- Never invent a maker's name, price, story, or food licence. If you do not know it, leave the blank and ask.
- Never broadcast to makers as a generic shop list. Maker outreach and the Knock are 1:1, from a person.
- Never use a maker's photo or story without their ticked consent.
- Never show one maker's pricing or margin on a label. The shop-wide pledge is the only place pay is named.
- Never put chilled dairy, meat, or temperature-controlled food on the shelf at launch.
- Never sell or pay a maker through GHL. Square is the till.
- Never auto-climb a maker up a Membership Journey rung from an RSVP or a form. Rungs are moved by hand in the Monday sweep, earned by a real action.
- Never sell food before the council EHO call is confirmed in writing.
- Never use em-dashes, and never use "working bees" (say "work days"). Place names: Witta on Jinibara Country.

## When stuck

Ask Ben or Nic. The consignment split wording and the Square setup are theirs to confirm with Standard Ledger. Anything touching the community lane (Jinibara, elders, storytellers) is by hand, with consent, and goes to Ben or Nic first, always.
