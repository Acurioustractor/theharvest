# Shop: open hours, drop-off, restock, payouts

> The everyday running of the shop, written for a first-week steward. Short steps, plain words. When something is not decided yet, you will see a [DECIDE: ...] note. Do not guess past it.

**Who does this:** the stewards on shift (Susie and Joey anchor the open hours). Makers do occasional shifts in exchange for shelf space.

**The screen(s) you need:** Square POS (the till) for every sale, stock, and payout. WhatsApp for telling makers their thing sold. Paper on the wall for the roster and the drop-off label. You do not need GHL for selling. (Four screens plus paper is the whole system: GHL for relationships, Square for the till, Humanitix for events, WhatsApp for crews, Paper for the room.)

## A. Open hours

1. Hours start small. Weekend or market-day windows only to begin. They get longer only as the shelf and the foot traffic prove out. Do not open extra days on your own.
2. The current open hours are printed on the door and on the website. Follow what is on the door.
3. [DECIDE: the exact open-hour days and times for phase one. The docs lock "weekend or market-day windows" but do not name the hours.]
4. Open up: turn on the iPad or tablet, open the Square POS app, check the card reader is charged and paired.
5. Through the day: greet people, tell the maker stories if asked. This is a connection, not a checkout. Most people come to belong, not just to buy. That is fine.
6. Close up: put the tablet and the reader away on charge. Tidy the shelf. Leave the come-back sheet and any paper notes in the Monday pile.

## B. Drop-off (a maker brings stock in)

1. Drop-off happens in one fixed weekly window, the same time each week (a habit, not a booking). The window is printed on the wall.
2. [DECIDE: the exact drop-off day and time. The staff view gives "e.g. Thu 3 to 5pm" as an example only, not a locked time.]
3. When a maker arrives with stock, take the items and the kraft drop-off label they bring (or fill one with them).
4. Remember consignment: the maker keeps ownership of the item until it sells. We are holding it, not buying it. Low risk for the maker. They only get paid once it sells.
5. Add the items to the Square catalogue, one item per maker product. Put the maker's name in the item description or the SKU prefix (for example `SAR-` for Sarah's items) so the sales report can be split by maker later.
6. Make or update the shelf label for each item:
   - Item name, maker first name, property or business name.
   - Where it was made, and what it was made from.
   - The price.
   - Optional: one line of the maker's own words.
7. Never put the maker's individual cut or margin on the label. The shop-wide pledge board does the "what they got paid" talking. Each label just names the maker and the origin.
8. Place the items on the shelf.
9. Photograph the first shelf placement and send it to the maker that day: a one-line WhatsApp with the photo. That photo is what turns a one-off into a regular. This is staff work, not software, and it is never automated.

## C. Restock and keeping the shelf right

1. Watch what is selling and what is running low.
2. When a maker's stock runs low, this is a relationship touch, not a blast. Reach out to that one maker, one to one (a message, a call). Never send a generic "restock" message to all makers at once.
3. When new stock comes in, follow the drop-off steps (section B): catalogue it in Square, label it, photograph the first placement, message the maker.
4. Update a label whenever a maker's offer changes. This is part of the normal weekly rhythm, not a special job.
5. Food on the shelf, phase one, is only two things: pre-packaged shelf-stable food (preserves, jams, honey, dried, baked, ferments) made by a maker who holds their own food licence, and fresh whole fruit and vegetables. No chilled dairy, no meat, no ready-to-eat food needing temperature control. If a maker offers any of those, say not yet and check with Ben or Nic.
6. [DECIDE: written confirmation from the Sunshine Coast Council Environmental Health team on the phase-one food position, before the first food sale. This is a single phone call, owned by Ben or Nic.]

## D. Payouts (paying the maker their share)

1. The split: the maker keeps about 75 to 80 percent of what the customer pays. The shop keeps 20 to 25 percent, which covers card fees, staffing, and overhead.
2. [DECIDE: the exact split inside the 75 to 80 percent band, confirmed by Standard Ledger. Do not print a fixed percent on the pledge board until this is confirmed.]
3. We run payouts the Square way (Path A at launch): no consignment add-on, manual reconciliation by hand.
4. The rhythm: at the agreed time (the docs say weekly or monthly, with Square's own example being Monday morning looking back at the weekend's sales), one person does the run.
5. The run, step by step:
   - Export the sales report from the Square dashboard.
   - Split the sales by maker (this is why each item carries the maker's name or SKU prefix).
   - Pay each maker their share by bank transfer.
   - Send each maker a one-line "how it sold" note.
6. [DECIDE: payout cadence, weekly or monthly. The docs offer both. The staff-week rhythm suggests a month-end payout run; the Square setup suggests a Monday weekly reconciliation. Pick one and print it.]
7. We move to a paid consignment add-on (Path B) only when there are more than about 20 active makers, or when this reconciliation starts taking more than an hour a week. Reviewed at six months. Until then, stay on Path A.

## Never do (the box)

- Never sell a thing through anything but Square. Tickets go through Humanitix, belonging goes through the GHL link. Things on shelves go through Square, always.
- Never put a maker's individual price cut or margin on a label or sign. Only the shop-wide pledge names a percent.
- Never broadcast to makers as a generic shop list. Maker outreach and restock asks are one to one, in a defined order, from a person.
- Never automate the "it sold" photo message to a maker. A human sends it, that day.
- Never put high-risk food (chilled dairy, meat, ready-to-eat) on the shelf at launch.
- Never buy stock from a maker outright. It is consignment: they keep ownership until it sells.
- Never print a fixed pledge percent before Standard Ledger confirms the split.
- Never touch the community lane through software. Anything to do with Jinibara, elders, or storytellers, or any use of a community name, story, or photo, is by hand, with consent, and you ask Ben or Nic first.

## When stuck

Ask Ben or Nic. For anything about a community name, story, photo, or an elder, always ask first, before you act.
