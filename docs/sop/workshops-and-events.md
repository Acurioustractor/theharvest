# Workshops and events: create, invite, run, close the loop

**Who does this:** the steward running the day (Susie or Joey), with Ben building the first event templates and Nic on the gate. A volunteer taps names at the door.

**The screen(s) you need:** Humanitix organiser (the event and the check-in), WhatsApp (the crews), and Paper (the come-back sheet on the table). GHL is touched later, in the Monday sweep, not on the day.

This is one repeating loop. Every event, the same five steps: create, invite, remind, run, close the loop.

## Create the event

1. Open Humanitix organiser.
2. Find the matching template ("Work Day" or "Workshop"). Do not build from scratch.
3. Duplicate it and change the date. That is the whole job.
4. Set a cap if the room is tight (for example, the 18-seat maker session). Turn waitlists on so a full event still collects names.
5. Free or paid: both are fine. Humanitix handles GST and tickets. A free event costs nothing.
6. Keep it plain: one image, plain words, no marketing voice.

## Invite, circles first

7. Invite in this order: the crew WhatsApp group first, then members by email, then the public last (if at all). The people building the place hear first.
8. Ask for hands, not attendance. "We're building the kids' corner, who's in?" beats "come along".
9. The members' email and the makers' invite are broadcasts (you press send), built by Ben. They go to one named list, never "All". An RSVP or event yes never subscribes anyone to the newsletter.

## Remind

10. Humanitix sends the reminders and the day-before nudge by itself. You do nothing here.

## Run the day

11. Check people in on the Humanitix organiser app at the gate. A volunteer taps names. No paper headcount maths at 5pm.
12. Put the paper come-back / can-help sheet on the table. This is the relationship instrument: who came, who can help, what they can do.
13. Give everyone a small real job in the first ten minutes. A job on arrival is how a visitor becomes a maker.
14. Only photograph people who have said yes to a photo. In a small town an unconsented photo costs trust you cannot win back.

## Close the loop (this is the part that matters)

15. After the day, send the cleared photos to the crew WhatsApp group, and a one-line personal thank-you to people who showed up. A message from a person, not a template.
16. On Monday, the sweep does the rest: someone types the paper sheet into GHL, tags skills, and notes any `pod:` crew invites. Attendees land as an `event:<slug>-<date>` tag. That tag is a headcount only.
17. Move someone up a Membership Journey rung only because they DID something (helped, came back, contributed), and only by hand in the Monday sweep. An RSVP or an attendee tag is never a rung.

> **Never do**
> - Never build an event from scratch. Always duplicate a template and change the date.
> - Never read an RSVP, a tap, or an attendee tag as a lead or a rung. Nobody climbs automatically.
> - Never let an event yes subscribe anyone to the newsletter. Invite them to follow later, by choice.
> - Never send a broadcast that adds a tag (adding a tag fires a workflow and can spam a whole list).
> - Never use a photo, name, or story of anyone in the community lane (`lane:community` / `role:elder` / `role:storyteller`) without a yes at the time. That work is by hand, with consent, ask Ben or Nic first, always.
> - Never put the day's organising into new software. Rosters are a printed sheet on the wall plus a photo in WhatsApp. Software only when paper breaks.
> - No em-dashes, no marketing words (vibrant, tapestry, testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, seamless, delve). Say "work days", never "working bees". Witta is on Jinibara Country.
> - Never invent a count, a date, an attendance number, or a build claim. If the true thing is not ready, leave the blank.

**When stuck:** ask Ben (the event templates, the broadcasts, the Monday sweep) or Nic (the day itself, the gate). Anything touching elders, storytellers, or the community lane, ask Ben or Nic first, always.
