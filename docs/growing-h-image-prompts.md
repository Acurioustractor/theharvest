# The Growing H — Image Prompts

All images: **graphite pencil on warm cream toned paper.** Soft shading, hatching, cross-hatching, atmospheric perspective. Realistic proportions. No ink line art. No digital/clean vector look.

Canvas: 1:1 square (1024x1024) unless noted otherwise.

---

## THE H MARK — 6 LIFECYCLE STAGES

These are the hero images for each stage of the Growing H. The H is rendered as a living, organic mark — two posts (tree trunks) and three bars (branches). Each stage shows a different moment in its lifecycle.

### Stage 01 — The Seed

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A single small seed sitting in dark rich soil, seen from slightly above. The seed is almond-shaped, textured, with a tiny crack where a root tip is just visible. Around it, soft cross-hatched earth, small pebbles, organic matter. No plant yet. Just potential. The composition is centered and intimate, with generous negative space above the seed — the emptiness is the point. Atmospheric, quiet, still. The drawing should feel like a whisper.

**Purpose:** Hero image for Stage 01. The very beginning. Nothing built yet.
**Feeling:** Stillness. Potential. Trust.

---

### Stage 02 — The Shoots

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. Two thin seedling stems pushing up from dark soil, parallel, about a hand-width apart. No leaves yet, just pale woody shoots with the faintest suggestion of nodes where branches might come. The stems are slightly different heights — one a little ahead of the other. Fine root tendrils visible where the stems meet the earth. Soft hatching for the soil. The shoots catch light from one side, casting tiny shadows. Simple, hopeful, fragile. Generous negative space above.

**Purpose:** Hero image for Stage 02. The two posts emerging. People arriving before the doors open.
**Feeling:** Fragility. Emergence. Hope.

---

### Stage 03 — The Sapling

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. Two young tree trunks, about forearm-thick, growing from rich soil. Between them at the base, a single horizontal branch reaches across, connecting the two trunks — like a natural crossbar. The branch has a few small leaves. The trunks show bark texture starting to form, with subtle grain and knots. Small roots visible at ground level. Above the connecting branch, open space between the trunks where more branches will eventually grow. Dappled light. The drawing should feel like something taking hold.

**Purpose:** Hero image for Stage 03. The first bar (garden/grow) connects the two posts. Phase 1 infrastructure.
**Feeling:** Structure forming. Taking root. The first connection.

---

### Stage 04 — The Mature H

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. Two strong tree trunks forming the posts of an H shape, connected by three horizontal branches at different heights. The bottom branch has small leaves and herbs growing from it — green things (suggest with shading density). The middle branch is golden-toned heartwood, warm and smooth, with a sense of warmth radiating from it. The top branch is weathered, textured like rusted iron or aged timber, with a beautiful patina. The whole form is unmistakably an H but entirely organic. Full canopy of leaves at the top. Bark texture, depth, life. Strong cross-hatching for shadow. This is the primary logo state — mature, alive, full.

**Purpose:** Hero image for Stage 04. All three zones active. The complete H.
**Feeling:** Strength. Maturity. Alive and full.

---

### Stage 05 — The Fruit

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. The same mature H-shaped tree, but now things hang from the three horizontal branches. From the bottom branch: herbs, small vegetables, produce dangling on stems. From the middle branch: a lantern, a small pot, a ladle — the suggestion of a kitchen. From the top branch: a small framed canvas, a dangling mobile, children's artwork pinned with clips. Some items are detailed, others are loose gestural marks. The tree is abundant, generous, slightly wild. Leaves falling gently. A sense of overflow and generosity. The H is bearing fruit in every sense.

**Purpose:** Hero image for Stage 05. The H producing — residencies, meals, gardens, art.
**Feeling:** Abundance. Generosity. Harvest.

---

### Stage 06 — The Return

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. The H-shaped tree in late autumn or early winter. The two trunks remain solid and strong — unchanged. But the three horizontal branches are thinning, leaves falling. A scatter of fallen leaves, fruit, and organic matter at the base of the trunks, forming a soft mound of compost. Within that compost mound, a single small seed is visible — the same seed from Stage 01. The branches still hold their shape but are lighter, more transparent. Not death — transformation. The drawing should feel like an exhale, not an ending. Atmospheric perspective, soft edges.

**Purpose:** Hero image for Stage 06. Letting go. Composting. The cycle beginning again.
**Feeling:** Release. Transformation. The loop closing.

## THE COUNTRY — Jinibara

### Bunya Pine Gathering

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A towering bunya pine (Araucaria bidwillii) seen from below, looking up into its distinctive symmetrical canopy. The massive trunk rises through the centre of the composition. At the base of the tree, the suggestion of people gathered — not detailed faces, but silhouettes and forms sitting, standing, moving. The scale of the tree dwarfs the people. Atmospheric perspective pushes the background into soft haze. Cross-hatching builds the dense canopy. Bunya cones visible in the upper branches — large, football-sized. The feeling is ancient, sacred, communal. Blackall Range hinterland visible in the far background as soft rolling hills.

**Purpose:** The Jinibara Country section. Baroon Pocket bunya gatherings, thousands of years of custodianship.
**Feeling:** Ancient. Sacred. Scale.

---

### Shaun's Oysters — Full Cycle

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A close-up arrangement: several rough-textured oyster shells sitting on a surface of rammed earth. The earth shows its characteristic horizontal layers — compressed soil, gravel, subtle colour bands. One shell is open, the oyster visible. Next to the shells, a small scattering of crushed shell fragments being pressed into the earth surface. The composition tells the story of return — shells from Moreton Bay going into the ground at Witta. Intimate, textured, tactile. Heavy use of cross-hatching for the shell ridges and earth grain.

**Purpose:** Shaun Fisher's Aboriginal oyster farming story. Shells from Stradbroke Island returning to rammed earth.
**Feeling:** Return. Cycle. Connection between Country.

## THE PLACE — Barry's Ridge

### Barry's Shed and Crane

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A weathered industrial shed on a hilltop ridge, seen from a slight distance. A rusted crane arm extends from the shed roof, silhouetted against the sky. The shed is timber and corrugated iron, with decades of patina. Golden hour light falls across the scene from the left, catching the crane arm and the shed walls. Rammed earth walls visible on the lower portion of the building. Hinterland hills roll away in the background, soft with atmospheric perspective. Paddock grass in the foreground. A few scattered eucalyptus trees. The composition should feel monumental but quiet — a place that has been standing a long time.

**Purpose:** The origin. Barry's shed, the crane, the property at Witta.
**Feeling:** Heritage. Endurance. Golden hour.

---

### The Rammed Earth Walls

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. An extreme close-up of a rammed earth wall surface. The horizontal compression lines are clearly visible — layers of earth, sand, gravel compressed into bands. Small stones and aggregate visible in cross-section. A warm, golden-toned surface with subtle colour variation between layers. A hand is placed flat against the wall, giving scale. The wall is warm to the touch — you can feel the decades of sun stored in it. Strong directional light from one side creates long shadows in the texture. Detailed hatching and stippling for the earth grain.

**Purpose:** The material. What the posts of the H are made of. The building itself.
**Feeling:** Warmth. Solidity. Time stored in material.

## THE ZONES — Three Bars

### Bottom Bar — The Garden (Grow)

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A raised garden bed built from rough timber, overflowing with herbs and leafy greens. Basil, rosemary, lettuce, kale — identifiable but drawn with soft, organic marks rather than botanical precision. Soil visible between plants, rich and dark. A garden fork leans against the bed frame. Morning dew suggested with tiny highlight marks. A pathway of crushed gravel leads past the bed. In the background, soft suggestion of a polytunnel structure. The feeling is productive, generous, accessible — anyone could pick from this bed.

**Purpose:** The garden zone. Bottom bar of the H. Earth element.
**Feeling:** Growth. Abundance. Accessibility.

---

### Middle Bar — The Kitchen (Feed)

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A long communal timber table seen from one end, stretching into the middle distance. Bowls of soup, torn bread, scattered herbs, a large pot at the centre. Mismatched chairs and stools. No people visible but their presence is everywhere — a jacket draped over a chair back, a half-eaten bowl, a child's drawing tucked under a plate edge. Rammed earth walls visible on one side. Natural light pouring through a large window. The table has years of use in its grain. Steam rises from the pot. The feeling is warmth, sustenance, gathering.

**Purpose:** The kitchen zone. Middle bar of the H. Water element.
**Feeling:** Sustenance. Warmth. The shared table.

---

### Top Bar — The Art Space (Make)

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. An open studio space with high ceilings and exposed timber beams. A large canvas on an easel, partially painted. A workbench with tools — brushes, clay, wire, scissors. Children's artwork pinned to a line strung between beams. A potter's wheel in one corner. Natural light from skylights above. The rusted crane arm visible through a high window, connecting to Barry's heritage. The space is messy in the right way — creative, in-use, alive. Not a gallery but a workshop where things are being made.

**Purpose:** The art space zone. Top bar of the H. Air element.
**Feeling:** Making. Exchange. Creative energy.

## THE PEOPLE

They are not posed — one is pointing at something in the distance, one holds a garden tool, one has plans or papers rolled under an arm, one stands with hands on hips. ## SUPPLEMENTARY / TEXTURE

### The Milk Crate Pavilion

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A structure made from stacked plastic milk crates — hundreds of them, interlocking, forming walls and a canopy. The crates create a pattern of light and shadow as sun passes through the grid. People sitting underneath on simple benches. A child climbing the outside. A string of festoon lights woven through the crate structure. The pavilion sits on grass with the shed visible behind. The drawing captures the DIY, community-built, impermanent quality — this is something anyone could have helped build.

**Purpose:** The milk crate pavilion from Phase 1. Community-built gathering anchor.
**Feeling:** Resourceful. Community-built. Impermanent by design.

---

### Compost Cycle (Detail)

> Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper. A circular composition showing the stages of decomposition and regrowth. Starting at the top and moving clockwise: a fresh leaf, a wilting leaf, a decomposing leaf fragment, dark humus, a tiny root emerging, a seedling shoot, a small leaf unfurling, back to the fresh leaf. The centre of the circle is empty — negative space. Each stage is drawn with appropriate texture — the fresh leaf crisp and detailed, the decomposition increasingly rough and textured, the new growth tender and fine. The drawing is scientific in its observation but poetic in its composition.

**Purpose:** The Return stage. The cycle. Compost feeding new growth.
**Feeling:** Cycle. Nothing is wasted. Transformation.

## GENERATION NOTES

**Model:** Gemini 2.0 Flash or Imagen 3
**Style prefix for all:** "Realistic graphite pencil drawing on warm cream toned paper."
**Negative prompts (if supported):** No ink lines, no digital art, no vector, no watercolour, no colour (graphite only — tonal values, not hue), no cartoon, no clipart
**Resolution:** 1024x1024 minimum
**Post-processing:** May need slight warm cream tint overlay if output is pure white background

### Generation Order (recommended)
1. Stage 04 (Mature H) — this is the primary mark, nail this first
2. Stage 01 (Seed) — simplest, establishes the style
3. Stage 06 (Return) — bookend with Stage 01
4. Stage 02, 03, 05 — fill in the growth sequence
5. Barry's Shed — the origin image
6. Bunya Pine — the Country image
7. Zone images (Garden, Kitchen, Art Space) — support material
8. Everything else — texture and supplementary
