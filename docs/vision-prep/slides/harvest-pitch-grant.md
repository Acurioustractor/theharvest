# The Harvest

A place the community doesn't have yet — where art and food connect people with themselves and each other.

Witta, Sunshine Coast Hinterland

---

# Why The Harvest

## Three principles that guide everything we build

**Everything is art.** We treat every surface, every meal, every interaction as something worth making beautiful.

**Nothing is permanent.** Like a gallery — exhibitions come, people love them, they evolve. The space is always becoming.

**Community-built.** The kids build the kids area. The community runs the milk bar. People feel ownership because they actually own it.

> This isn't a restaurant that might fail. It's a living space that adapts.

---

# The Site

## Six zones, each with its own identity

| Zone | Heritage | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| **The Garden** | Cooperative | Food sovereignty — everything we serve starts here |
| **The Pavilion** | Timber | Outdoor gathering — drinks, conversation, making |
| **The Kids Area** | Cooperative | Families and play — kids design and reshape it |
| **The Milk Bar** | Dairy | Affordable, playful — the community's front door |
| **The Gallery** | Timber | Ticketed art — curated exhibitions, artist residencies |
| **The Kitchen** | Dairy | High-end seasonal dining — Stage 2 with Sean's input |

Three heritage threads tie it together: **Timber** (heat, fire, making), **Dairy** (milk, fuel, nourishment), **Cooperative** (mutual aid, community).

# Garden + Pavilion

<div class="zone-pair">
<div class="zone">

### The Garden

**Why:** Food sovereignty. Everything we serve comes from here.

**What:** Market garden, raised beds, established plants. Barry spent 27 years restoring this soil — tomatoes, coffee, herbs, pecan trees.

**How:** Gardener starts mid-Feb, 2 days/week. Planting immediately. First produce by March.

</div>
<div class="zone">

### The Pavilion

**Why:** An outdoor space to convene — drinks, conversation, making.

**What:** 30sqm timber deck under the pecan trees. Scaffold structure. Flexible — markets one day, music the next.

**How:** Asphalt base exists. Deck + scaffold, milk crate furniture. ~$5-8K.

</div>
</div>

# Kids Area + Milk Bar

<div class="zone-pair">
<div class="zone">

### The Kids Area

**Why:** Families need somewhere for kids. Kids build it themselves — that's the art.

**What:** Cordoned section with mulch, play structures, rotating workshops. Leah runs quarterly sessions where kids literally reshape the space.

**How:** Clean the area, lay mulch. First kids activation May.

</div>
<div class="zone">

### The Milk Bar

**Why:** Affordable, playful, dairy heritage. Not a cafe — a milk bar.

**What:** Bright colours, retro vintage, radio playing. Fun, approachable, affordable food and drinks.

**How:** Expression of interest to community — "who wants to run the milk bar?" Give them a month and a budget to build the interior.

</div>
</div>

<div class="zone-pair">
<div class="zone">

### The Gallery / Shed

**Why:** Art that belongs to the space. Ticketed, curated, high-end.

**What:** Timber floors, curated exhibitions. Artist in residence builds work for the gallery. Rusted iron cladding, copper lights.

**How:** Clad the shed (~$5-10K). First artist residency: Ben and Nick. Thais designing concepts.

### The Kitchen / Restaurant

**Why:** High-end food experience. Seasonal menu that changes everything — cutlery, plates, decor, the lot.

**What:** Modular kitchen in the rammed earth building. Multiple rooms, cold room, high-quality bones.

**How:** Stage 2. Sean to advise on layout, capacity, positioning. Separate detailed conversation.
