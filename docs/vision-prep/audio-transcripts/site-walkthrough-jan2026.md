## Summary of Walkthrough

### The Site
- Former nursery ("Green Harvest") — ~500sqm building + gardens + outdoor spaces
- Soil is excellent from years of plant/seed growing
- Previous owner grew unique plants, let it overgrow after burnout
- 2,000+ cars pass on the road every weekend (Kenilworth traffic)
- Rammed earth building is high quality — cool in summer, warm in winter
- Cold room, seed store, workshop areas, shade house all existing
- Bioretention/septic system on site
- 20-30 car parks existing
- Brazilian coffee trees growing on property
- Pomegranate, tomatoes, curry leaf, lilly pillies, bush tomatoes, pecan trees, mulberry, taro all on site

### Design Principles Discussed
1. **Gallery/exhibition model** — nothing super permanent, intentional rotation like an art gallery
2. **Flexible/modular** — structures that can be reconfigured, pulled down, rebuilt
3. **"Alice in Wonderland"** — multiple experiences you stumble into, surprise and delight
4. **Community canvas** — not finished, people come to be part of creating it
5. **Nature-first** — bring people out to nature, picking rocket from the garden
6. **Accessible but artistic** — creative and different but not intimidating
7. **Test before investing** — pop-ups before permanent builds

### Key Zones Identified

#### Zone 1: Front Garden / Outdoor Space
- Scaffold structure with galvanized/rusted pipe (not a marquee, not permanent)
- Clear laserlight panels for rain protection while keeping light
- Paths with decomposed granite/gravel
- Raised gardens with timber or milk crates
- Living tables from ACT Farm (plants growing from tables)
- Hammock/reading nooks between trees
- Kids area with climbing structures, fairy garden
- Fire pit area with seating under pecan trees
- Water feature / pond element (missing water on site)
- Entry from road needs strong visual attraction — crane, sculpture, neon sign

#### Zone 2: The Shed (Stage 1 Priority)
- Clad with rusted iron or plywood — "5-10 sheets makes it look like an old barn"
- Replace fluoro lights with copper hanging lights
- Could be retail/deli serving space
- Container repositioned alongside, connected with covering
- Pizza oven from farm, possible tuk-tuk
- Sink/washing area for self-service cooking
- Budget: $5-10K for materials + labour

#### Zone 3: Shade House
- Remove shade cloth, make it a greenhouse/hangout
- Tables underneath with plants growing over
- Connected to garden experience
- Could rebuild the "seed tunnel" (has existing approval)

#### Zone 4: Main Building Interior
- Demolish internal walls to create one modular rectangle
- Performance space / restaurant / making space — reconfigurable
- Lowering truss system (stage design concept) for intimacy + lighting
- Kitchen location: behind wall with servery window (Sean's restaurant friend's idea)
- Or: open fire cooking from garden side
- Hide PVC ceiling — spray black or drop fabric/sling panels
- Columns are structural — design around them
- Windows are key light source — make them features
- Rusted laser-cut "The Harvest" sign with lighting

#### Zone 5: Back Rooms
- "The Classroom" — bookable education/workshop space
- Seed store = storage for classroom equipment (on wheels)
- Reception area at entry
- Could host: Pilates, pottery, design classes, birthing education, cooking
- Rammed earth room = artist in residence office/studio

#### Zone 6: Bathrooms (Critical Decision)
- Option A: Temporary toilet block brought in
- Option B: Keep existing bathroom, wall off corridor, door from outside
- Option C: Convert rooms into 2+ bathrooms (need disabled access)
- Option D: Use wine bar toilets next door (Stage 1 workaround)
- Need: 1 disabled + 1 regular minimum (1 per 50 people rule)
- Sewer/septic is accessible — trench to container or cut floor

### Materials / Aesthetic
- **Timber theme**: pays homage to local timber/sawmill industry
- **Milk crate theme**: dairy industry heritage — bright, playful, Alice in Wonderland
- **Rammed earth**: Thais's expertise, using oyster shells in flooring
- **Rusted iron/galvanized pipe**: industrial but warm
- Scaffolding structures with hanging elements
- Milk bottle chandeliers (Dutch Design Week reference)
- Earth pavers with shells — polished looks like marble
- Mismatched community furniture (everyone brings a chair)
- Wind chime sculpture on crane (community contributes metal objects)

### Community Engagement Strategy
- First event: free, collect emails for mailing list
- Ask community: "What do you want to offer? What's your dream?"
- Community directory of skills and interests
- Kids paid to pick produce from gardens
- Offerings/exchange stall on roadside (like the bouquet lady)
- WhatsApp community feed
- Facebook local page marketing
- Ticketed events to test demand before investing
- Funnel: free → $20 pop-up → annual subscription → boutique experiences

### Thais's Design Observations (end of day)
- Entry should be from cafe side, service entry separate
- 14m x 9m pavilion area fits under big trees
- Shade is critical — afternoon sun from north
- Container should move to align with shed, create bigger covered area
- Kids area near dense bush (contained, safe)
- Hammock/reading pockets between trees
- Garden beds in center with tables between
- Path from stairs (8 steps) down to main building corridor
- Road frontage needs strong visual draw — something artistic at fence line
- Greenhouse structure should be restored
- Water element is missing — pond, water play, flowing feature
- Multiple concurrent activities should feel complementary not chaotic
- Wheelchair accessibility needs consideration in path design
