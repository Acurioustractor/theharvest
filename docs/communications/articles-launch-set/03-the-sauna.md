We are thinking about a sauna.

That sentence is the whole story. Nothing has been built. There are no permits drawn. There are no quotes accepted. But it is honest enough to say in public, because that is how this place keeps making decisions: out loud, with the community, before too much money gets committed.

Here is the thinking.

### The climate is the argument

Witta sits at five hundred and sixty-one metres on the Blackall Range. Howells Knob, just up the road, is the highest point on the range. We are five to ten degrees cooler than the coast on most days, and a lot wetter. Two thousand and thirty-seven millimetres of rain a year. Mist sits on the ridge for hours after the sun is up.

That is not a complaint. It is the reason the rainforest grew here. It is the reason the dairy industry chose this land. It is the reason the garden we are planting will be productive. But it is also the reason that, in winter, sitting outside is hard work, and being inside without a way to get warm together is a different kind of hard.

A sauna is, fundamentally, a small heated room. In a hinterland that gets cold, wet and misty for six months of the year, a small heated room shared by neighbours is a piece of useful, low-energy infrastructure.

### The history is the argument too

Witta was named Teutoburg by the families who settled here in 1887, all from Brisbane's Logan district, all of German origin. Communal warmth was not a niche imported idea here. It was the operating system the village arrived with. The name was changed during the First World War to "Witta" — from the Kabi word *wetya*, meaning dingo — to defuse anti-German sentiment, but the families and the practices stayed.

The cooperative tradition that built Maleny's first butter factory in 1904 was the same tradition that built the village halls, the saw co-ops, the shared equipment lists. Pooled resources for the practices that work better when shared.

A sauna is co-op infrastructure for the body. Same logic, smaller scale.

### What we are picturing

A small wood-fired sauna. Sized for six to eight people at once. **Cedar-lined** (see [The Cedar](/blog/the-cedar) for where the wood comes from). Sitting close enough to the garden that you can come from a Wednesday Maintenance Crew shift, sit in the heat for ten minutes, and walk back into the kitchen feeling new. With a cold-water plunge or rinse outside.

Bookable by members. Open at scheduled community sessions. Maybe one Sunday morning a fortnight.

It is the first piece we are likely to build using Harvest revenue rather than grant funding. That is on purpose. The grant pieces (the pavilion, the residencies, the cultural protocols) make the place legible. The sauna is the first piece we want to fund ourselves, because it is the first piece that is **for us** rather than **for the work**.

### What we don't know yet

Lots.

- Where exactly it sits on the site.
- Whether the heater is wood-fired or electric. Wood is more honest. Electric is easier on the council DA.
- Whether it is one room or two (the European pattern is sauna + cool room; the Japanese pattern is hot + cold + a bench between them).
- Who in the community wants to be involved in designing it.
- Whether we hire a builder or build it ourselves with a sauna-experienced community member leading.

If you have an opinion or, better, an experience with this kind of project, [we'd like to hear it](mailto:hello@theharvestwitta.com.au?subject=Sauna%20%E2%80%94%20I%20have%20thoughts).

### What this article is

It is a public draft. The Harvest tries to think out loud where it can. Some pieces have answers (the pavilion, the cedar work, the garden). Some pieces are still questions (the sauna, the shop, the seasonal residency calendar). It feels right to publish both kinds.

If a small heated room shared with neighbours sounds like a useful thing on Jinibara Country at five hundred and sixty metres, you are part of the audience this is being written for. Tell us what you would want it to be.
