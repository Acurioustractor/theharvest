We needed a roof before we needed walls.

That's not a slogan, it's a constraint. The Harvest, on Jinibara Country at Witta, is a long project. The buildings will take their time. The garden grows on its own clock. But the community has been waiting for two years, and a community needs somewhere to gather before the architecture gives them a room.

So we built the pavilion first. In a weekend. With eighty of you.

### What it is

The Milk Create Pavilion is a modular open-air structure under the pecan trees. Roughly fourteen metres by nine. Scaffold poles for the bones. Reclaimed milk crates from the dairy industry for the cladding. Salvaged timber for everything else. It can be a gallery, a long-table dining room, a film screen, a market hall, sometimes all in the same afternoon.

It comes apart. It rearranges. It will keep changing as we learn what we actually use it for.

### About the name

People called it "the milk crate pavilion" while it was being built. Descriptive, literal, accurate. We kept that name and renamed it at the same time. **The Milk Create Pavilion.**

Same beginning, single letter swap. Milk crate, milk create. The first one is what it is built from. The second one is what it does. Both true at once. The rename keeps the dairy heritage of the village in the name of the structure and adds what the structure does now. We make things here. The pavilion is the place where the making happens before there is anywhere else to put it.

### Why milk crates

The crate is the icon of this place. From 1904, when Maleny's first butter factory opened, until dairy deregulation in 2000, this hinterland ran on the co-operative dairy model. Stacked, shared, returned, restacked. One in eight Queenslanders lived on a dairy farm at the industry's peak. The crates carried the milk that built every house on this ridge.

When deregulation came in, the floor prices guaranteed for generations vanished overnight. Farms that sustained families walked off. The crates went to landfill, to back paddocks, to nothing.

We took the form and made it a roof. Not as nostalgia. As repurpose. Witta keeps living off what Witta has, even when the economy that produced it is gone.

### Who built it

The pavilion is the field output of the **Radical Scoops** fellowship from Regional Arts Australia. The fellowship asked: what does art do when it is asked to make agriculture, food, and community legible to itself? The pavilion was the answer.

But the fellowship paid for the materials. The pavilion was made by:

- Eighty community members across one weekend in March 2026
- Local builders who came down to teach knots and bracing
- Children who carried single crates twice their size
- Susie and Joey, our incoming Community Stewards, who fed everyone

If you were there, your name is in the floor.

### What's underneath it now

The pavilion has already hosted:

- The first community dinner on the site
- The kids' co-design afternoon for the art space
- A Wednesday Maintenance Crew lunch
- An informal artists' meeting for the upcoming Creative Australia residencies

It will host the **first soft opening on Saturday 20 June 2026, member list only**. A small group, simple food, and a short circle about what comes next. The bigger public day lands later in the year. If you didn't help build the pavilion, the member list is how you get under it.

### Why this is the first piece in the collection

The Harvest is being made as a slow museum. Each part is a piece. The garden is a piece. The cedar we are working with is a piece. The shop and the sauna are pieces that haven't happened yet.

The pavilion is the piece we needed to make first because it makes everything else possible. A community can't co-design a place it can't gather in. So we built the gathering first, and now we get to keep building everything else underneath it.

If you want to come and see what gathering it sits over, join the member list. The first invite goes there.
