# The Harvest — Brand and Voice Guide (start here for contributors)

> For volunteers, marketing interns, and anyone making content for The Harvest.
> This is the doc you read before you write or post anything. Created 2026-05-26.
> It orients you and points to the detail docs. It does not repeat them.

## Why you have this

The Harvest gives small, owned pieces of work to people it trusts, then checks the work. This guide is what makes that possible. If you own a mini-project (a story, a post series, a one-pager, a short video), this tells you the mission, the voice, the hard rules, and where things live, so you can move without waiting on Ben for every decision. Ben holds the vision and reviews the output. You hold the piece.

## The Harvest in three lines

The Harvest is a community hub at Witta, on Jinibara Country in the Sunshine Coast hinterland. The front door is three rooms: a garden, events, and an art space. It is being made in the open, with the people who turn up, on the site of a decades-old plant nursery called The Green Harvest.

## The one principle behind everything

People keep connecting because they get value and have agency, not because they are funnelled. Make things so useful and alive that people pull them toward their own goals and bring others. Every piece you make should give the receiver something they can use. If it does not, it does not go out. Full reasoning: `docs/strategy/community-engagement-launch-plan.md`.

## The voice: field notes, not marketing

Write like a note from the place to a neighbour. Plain, specific, grounded, generous. Short sentences. Real detail from a real day. The reader should feel close to the work and clear about one next useful thing.

Good:
- "The shed is starting to feel like a room again."
- "Barry has been walking us through what is already growing."
- "If you have a folding table or a few hours, reply to this email."

Not this:
- "We are thrilled to announce our exciting journey."
- "Join our vibrant community and be part of something special."

## Hard rules (do not break these)

1. **No made-up facts.** Never invent counts, dates, attendance numbers, or claims that something is finished. If a build is in progress, say in progress. Verify before writing anything in past tense. (See `feedback-no-fabricated-facts` in project memory.)
2. **No em-dashes.** Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses. This is firm across all Harvest writing.
3. **No AI or marketing vocabulary.** Banned: vibrant, tapestry, testament, underscore, pivotal, crucial, leverage, journey, thrilled, dive in, unlock, elevate, game-changer.
4. **Say "work days," never "working bees."**
5. **Place names.** Use Indigenous names, with colonial names in brackets where helpful. Witta and Jinibara Country are named, not decoration.
6. **Consent before publishing people.** Never publish someone's story, photo, or name without their consent. If consent is pending, it stays out.
7. **One call to action per piece.** If everything is important, nothing is.
8. **Real photos only.** From the property. Never stock, never AI-photorealistic.

Before you call a piece done, search it for em-dashes and the banned words.

## What you are feeding: the engagement ladder

People move up by choice as value adds up. Know which rung your piece serves:

- **Receive** — a story they enjoy and share. No ask.
- **Show up** — an invitation to a work day, event, or visit.
- **Use it** — something useful for their own goal (a guide, a recipe, a photo set, a space to host their own thing).
- **Make it** — a way to own a piece (co-design, run a workshop, steward a bed).
- **Hold it** — become a member or steward.

Most public content serves Receive and Show up. Always make sure there is a real next step, and never push someone up a rung they have not chosen.

## How a mini-project works

1. You get a brief: the goal, the rung it serves, the audience, the format, the deadline, and what done looks like.
2. You make it in draft.
3. You self-check against this guide (voice, hard rules, one CTA, consent).
4. Ben reviews. Trust and verify: you are trusted to make it, the work is checked before it ships.
5. It ships through the right surface (below), and the status is updated where the work lives.

## Where things live (do not build parallel surfaces)

- Strategy and the why: the repo, `docs/strategy/`.
- The roadmap (what and when): Notion Actions DB, Harvest view, records prefixed "Engagement:".
- People and audience: Notion Harvest Engagement DB plus GoHighLevel tags.
- Sending (email, social): GoHighLevel. Never draft content in GHL; draft in Notion.
- Capture (signups, messages): the website forms.

Edit a thing where it lives. Never write the same information in two places.
