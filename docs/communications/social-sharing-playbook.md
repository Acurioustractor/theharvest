# Social Sharing Playbook

## Main Channels

Instagram is for feeling the place.

Facebook is for helping local people know what is happening and what to do next.

Use both, but do not force the same caption to do the same job everywhere.

## Instagram Pattern

Best for:
- site photos
- beautiful details
- people and process
- historical Witta photos with clear attribution
- short reflections
- event atmosphere
- maker, garden, kitchen, and art space moments

Structure:

1. One clear opening line.
2. Two to five short paragraphs.
3. A concrete detail from the photo or moment.
4. A question or invitation.
5. Link direction only if useful.

Example shape:

```text
The shed is starting to feel awake.

Today was less about finishing anything and more about seeing what the place is ready for.

Good light through the side door. Old timber. Enough room for a long table, a few tools, and a messy first workshop.

What would you make here?

#TheHarvestWitta #Witta #SunshineCoastHinterland
```

## Facebook Page Pattern

Best for:
- events
- practical updates
- local announcements
- calls for help
- community stories
- local history asks and source notes
- longer context

Structure:

1. Clear news line.
2. Who, what, when, where.
3. Why it matters.
4. What people should do.
5. Link.

Example shape:

```text
We are opening the gates this Saturday.

Saturday 7 March, 11am.
9 Gumland Drive, Witta.

Come see the site, meet a few neighbours, share food, and help shape what this place becomes.

Kids welcome. Dogs on leads. Bring a chair or blanket.

Details: theharvestwitta.com.au/gather
```

## Facebook Group Pattern

Use groups carefully. The post should feel useful, local, and specific.

Best for:
- genuine local invitations
- asking for photos, stories, materials, or help
- asking people to identify historical photos or add local memory
- events that are actually open to the community

Avoid:
- brand-building language
- repeated reposts
- anything that feels like advertising

Group post formula:

```text
Hi Witta locals, quick invite.

[What is happening]

[Date, time, place]

[Why locals might care]

[What to bring or how to join]

Thanks, Ben and Nic
```

## Caption Types

| Type | Job | Best Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place note | Make people feel the site | Instagram |
| Practical update | Tell locals what is happening | Facebook |
| Story spotlight | Build trust through people | Instagram and Facebook |
| Event invite | Drive attendance | Facebook, Instagram, groups |
| Call for help | Ask for one concrete contribution | Facebook, groups |
| Behind the scenes | Show progress without overclaiming | Instagram stories |
| Newsletter teaser | Move people to email | Instagram and Facebook |
| History thread | Connect a real archive photo to a current Harvest work | Instagram and Facebook |

## Real Media Rule

Do not use AI-generated images for Harvest social posts.

Use real photos, video stills, screenshots, floor plans, historical images, or simple supporting icons. If a historical image is used, include a short source or attribution line in the caption.

## Hashtags

Keep them light. Five is usually enough.

Core:
- `#TheHarvestWitta`
- `#Witta`
- `#Maleny`
- `#SunshineCoastHinterland`
- `#CommunityBuilding`

Topic tags:

- `#Makerspace`
- `#HinterlandFood`
- `#LocalProducers`
- `#NativeNursery`

## Posting Rhythm

Normal week:
- Tuesday: place note or story
- Thursday: practical update or call for help
- Saturday or Sunday: community story or event reminder

Event week:
- 7 days out: event announcement
- 3 days out: practical reminder
- day before: short reminder
- day of: Instagram stories
- day after: thank-you post and next step
