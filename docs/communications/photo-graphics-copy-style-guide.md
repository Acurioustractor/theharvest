# Photo, Graphics, and Copy Style Guide

## Visual Principle

Show the real place first.

Graphics should support the story. Photos should carry the feeling. Copy should make the action clear.

AI-generated images are not Harvest brand or social assets. Use AI for cropping, layout options, captions, and sequencing real material only.

## Photo Rules

Use photos when the post is about:

- the site
- people
- a gathering
- materials
- food
- garden work
- process
- progress
- Witta history, where the source and rights are clear

Good photos:
- feel specific to Witta
- include hands, tools, soil, tables, signs, sheds, food, or faces where consent is clear
- have enough light to read well on a phone
- show what is actually happening
- carry a clear source note when they are historical

Avoid:
- AI-generated scenes, people, interiors, farms, or history
- generic landscape mood shots
- dark or blurry images where nothing is readable
- over-cropped details with no context
- photos of children without clear consent
- photos that imply a finished venue when the place is still emerging

## Graphic Rules

Use graphics when the post needs:

- event date and time
- countdown
- zone introduction
- clear callout
- Facebook cover
- story reminder
- a simple icon or label to support a real photo

Graphic hierarchy:

1. One headline.
2. One real image, plan, screenshot, or historical image.
3. One date or action.
4. Logo or name only if needed.

Do not put paragraph copy inside the graphic. Put the paragraph in the caption.

## Copy Voice

Write like someone standing at the gate, not like a campaign.

Use:
- plain sentences
- concrete details
- local references
- one clear invitation
- short paragraphs

Avoid:
- hype
- corporate launch language
- vague community words without evidence
- polished venue language
- overexplaining the philosophy before naming the thing

## First-Line Patterns

Useful first lines:

- The shed is starting to feel awake.
- Witta locals, this one is for you.
- We need a few hands this Saturday.
- Barry has been keeping this nursery alive for 27 years.
- The first table does not need to be perfect.
- Come see what is taking shape.

Weak first lines:

- We are excited to announce
- Join us on our journey
- Community is at the heart of everything we do
- The Harvest is proud to present

## The Three-Zone Lens

Every story should usually sit in one of these:

Garden:
- nursery
- soil
- native plants
- growing food
- working bees
- seasonal care

Kitchen:
- shared meals
- local producers
- pop-ups
- simple food moments
- long tables

Art Space:
- workshops
- making
- repair
- residencies
- local artists
- materials and tools

If a post does not fit one zone, it is probably:
- a whole-site update
- a people story
- a call for help
- an event invitation

## Asset Naming

Use this pattern for new assets:

```text
YYYY-MM-DD-channel-topic-format.ext
```

Examples:

```text
2026-05-02-instagram-shed-working-bee-square.jpg
2026-05-02-facebook-working-bee-landscape.png
2026-05-07-newsletter-garden-update-hero.jpg
```

## Consent and Care

Before publishing:

- confirm names and spelling
- check consent for identifiable people
- check consent before publishing children
- do not expose private phone numbers, emails, addresses beyond public event addresses, or sensitive operational details
- name Country with care when land or cultural story is involved
