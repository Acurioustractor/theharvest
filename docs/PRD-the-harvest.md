## 1. What Is The Harvest

The Harvest is a community space forming at 9 Gumland Drive, Witta — a 5-acre property on the Blackall Range, Sunshine Coast Hinterland, on Jinibara Country.

It is not a restaurant, not a gallery, not a farm. It's a place where art and food connect people with themselves and each other. Everything we build here follows three principles:

1. **Nothing is permanent.** Like a gallery — exhibitions come, people love them, they evolve. The space is always becoming.
2. **Community-built.** We don't build for people. We build with them.
3. **Custodianship.** We build to hand over.

The property sits on land cared for by the Jinibara people. The red soil has hosted timber workers and dairy farmers for over a century. Barry Rodgerig has lived next door since 1972. The cooperative movement that built Maleny's Credit Union and Maple Street Co-op runs through this ridge like a thread.

We're not starting from nothing.

## 2. Why Now

Three things have converged:

1. **The site is ready.** Lease signed, property accessible, gardens planted, infrastructure in place for outdoor events.
2. **Regional Arts Australia funding.** $45,000 grant for "Radical Scoops" — a project exploring the dairy, timber, and co-op heritage of the Blackall Range through photography, oral histories, and building things from recycled materials.
3. **Community momentum.** Neighbours asking questions. Homeschooling families circling. Local makers wanting in. Barry telling stories. Shaun Fisher — Quandamooka man, oyster farmer — saying yes.

The first public event is **Saturday 7 March 2026**. Everything we build for launch serves one question: *Should I come to this thing?*

### Overview

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|

| **Grant** | $45,000 |
| **Project name** | Radical Scoops |
| **Theme** | Dairy, timber, and co-op heritage of the Blackall Range |
| **Methods** | Photography, oral histories, recycled material builds, community storytelling |
| **Duration** | 2026 calendar year |

### What It Means in Practice

The Regional Arts funding isn't a separate project running alongside The Harvest — it IS the first chapter of The Harvest. The activities overlap completely:

1. **Photography & storytelling** — Documenting Barry, Shaun, the land, the neighbours. This becomes the Compendium (already live on the website at `/compendium`).
2. **Recycled material builds** — The milk crate pavilion at March 7 is the first physical deliverable. Built from donated dairy industry crates — heritage material, community labour.
3. **Oral histories** — Conversations with long-time residents about the ridge's dairy, timber, and cooperative past. Barry is the first. We're looking for more.
4. **Community engagement** — Every gathering is a Regional Arts activation. We don't do "events" and then separately do "art projects." They're the same thing.

## 6. The Website

### Purpose

The website serves two jobs:

1. **Before events:** Drive people to show up. Answer "should I come?"
2. **Between events:** Tell the story. Answer "what is this place?"

It is NOT a booking platform, a shop, a blog, or a content hub (yet). It's a landing page for a place that's becoming.

| Route | Page | Job |
|-------|------|-----|

## 7. The Six Zones (Full Vision)

The Harvest is organised into six zones, each tied to a heritage thread:

| Zone | Heritage | Purpose | Stage |
|------|----------|---------|-------|
| **The Garden** | Cooperative | Food sovereignty — everything served starts here | Active (planted) |
| **The Pavilion** | Timber | Outdoor gathering — drinks, conversation, making | Stage 1 (March) |
| **The Kids Area** | Cooperative | Families and play — kids design and reshape it | Stage 1 |
| **The Milk Bar** | Dairy | Affordable, playful — the community's front door | Stage 2 |
| **The Gallery** | Timber | Ticketed art — curated exhibitions, artist residencies | Stage 2 |
| **The Kitchen** | Dairy | High-end seasonal dining — modular, changes everything | Stage 3 |

Three heritage threads tie it together: **Timber** (heat, fire, making), **Dairy** (milk, fuel, nourishment), **Cooperative** (mutual aid, community).

**For March 7, only the outdoor zones activate:** Garden, Pavilion area, Kids Area (informal).

## 8. Staging

### Stage 1: Activate the Outside (Now – March)

Paths ripped in. Gardens planted. Milk crate pavilion built. First pop-up: oysters on the lawn.

**Budget:** ~$15–20K materials
**Revenue:** $0 (free event, pay-what-you-feel oysters)
**Goal:** Prove people show up. Capture community interest. Deliver first Regional Arts activation.

### Stage 2: Activate the Inside (April – June)

Milk bar fit-out (community-built). Gallery first exhibition. Kitchen planning. Toilets.

**Budget:** Additional ~$30–40K
**Goal:** Recurring programming. First revenue. Community operators identified.

### Stage 3: Full Operations (July onward)

Kitchen activated. Restaurant operating. Full team in place.

**Budget:** ~$200K total over 6 months to get fully open
**Goal:** $23K/month revenue. Break-even by Month 6. Community governance forming.

## 13. Principles for Building

These guide every decision about the website, the events, and the space:

1. **Invitation over explanation.** "Come eat oysters" beats "We're building a regenerative community space." Show, don't tell.
2. **People over program.** The event is the people who show up, not the schedule.
3. **Start scrappy, get beautiful.** Milk crates before marble. But make the milk crates beautiful.
4. **Support the people who say yes.** Shaun said yes — so we feature Shaun. Barry said yes — so we tell Barry's story. The website reflects who's actually in the room.
5. **Every section earns its place.** If a section on the website doesn't help someone decide to come or understand what this is, it goes.
