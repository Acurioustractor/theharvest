# Welcome over funnel: the grammar for The Harvest's public copy

> Written 2026-06-03 after Ben's note: the 20 June apparatus is a funnel, and a funnel teaches
> people they are being processed. This is the design doctrine and the rewritten page copy that
> makes the day feel like welcome, participation, fun and adventure, not data collection.
> Worked example: the `/june-20` page (`client/src/pages/GardenLaunch.tsx`). Voice: Curtis method,
> no em-dashes, no AI tells (`act-brand-alignment/references/writing-voice.md`).

## The principle

The day is a gift, not a funnel. You give it freely: open gate, free food, no ask, no reason
needed to come. Everything that captures (an RSVP tag, the shop EOI, an idea) sits underneath the
gift, small and quiet and optional. Most people who come should never enter a pipeline, and that
is the day working, not the day failing.

Two ACT frames hold this:
- **Beautiful obsolescence.** The Harvest becomes community-held, not ACT-dependent. So the copy
  hands the place to the town from the first line. "We hold the shelf for now. One day you won't
  need us to." The shop is theirs in waiting, not ours to recruit them into.
- **LCAA, Listen first.** The wall where people leave a thought is listening, not lead-capture. It
  belongs to the town, in the open, not siloed in a CRM. A thing only moves to a next step because
  a person asked for one.

## The grammar: from funnel to welcome

| Funnel grammar (cut) | Welcome grammar (use) |
|---|---|
| ask first ("tell us what you'd put on the shelf") | offer first ("walk the gardens, find the oven") |
| capture ("we write it all down") | witness ("there's a wall for it, no one's keeping score") |
| process ("three ways in", numbered steps) | come as you are ("you don't have to bring a thing") |
| in / out ("find out who's in, see if you are") | already in ("everyone's welcome, you don't need a reason") |
| convert (recruit them to the shop) | belong (they are neighbours, not leads) |
| future recruitment ("where we find out who's in") | present welcome ("the gate's open. come.") |
| verbs of form (fill, submit, register, tell us) | verbs of play (walk, find, meet, sit, stay, muck about) |

## Five rules for any Harvest public copy

1. **Lead with the gift.** The first thing a person meets is something given, no return expected.
2. **Every ask is an offer first.** Name "you don't owe us anything" before "here's where it goes."
3. **The smallest, quietest capture.** One tap. "We count the pizza by hand." Never a form when a
   tap will do, never a tap when nothing will do.
4. **Belonging is already true.** Nobody applies to be a neighbour. They turn up.
5. **It becomes theirs.** End on handover, not acquisition. The town will outgrow us, by design.

## The page, rewritten (current → new)

### Hero sub-line
- **Was:** "An old nursery on the hill becoming a place to eat, gather, make and grow. Come for the
  afternoon: walk the gardens, tell us what you'd put on the shop shelf, leave an idea, stay for
  pizza from five."
- **Now:** "An old nursery on the hill, opening up to be a place to eat, gather, make and grow.
  Come for the afternoon. Walk the gardens, find the oven under the pecans, meet whoever's there.
  Stay for pizza from five."
- **Was:** "Open day for anyone who might grow, make or gather here."
- **Now:** "Open day. Everyone's welcome, and you don't need a reason to come."

### What it is
- **Was:** "Come see the place. Walk the gardens. Tell us one thing: what you'd grow or make for the
  shop, how you'd use this space. / We write it all down, then the oven goes on under the pavilion."
- **Now:** "Come see the place. Walk the gardens. Sit at the long table. / If something comes to you,
  a thing you'd grow, a corner you'd use, there's a wall to put it on. Then the oven goes on under
  the pavilion."

### The shape of the day
- Keep. "Free. No register, no bar. Kids welcome, there's a corner with paper and chalk." This is
  already welcome grammar. The "Two questions" block softens from instruction to invitation:
  "If you feel like it, there are two questions on the wall" rather than a task to complete.

### Before the day (was "Three small ways in")
- **Header was:** "Three small ways in." (a three-step form)
- **Header now:** "Come as you are." with a first line that defuses the asks:
  "You don't have to bring a thing. Come empty-handed, that's the whole invitation. If you're the
  sort who likes to bring something, here's where it goes."
- The three cards become offers, not asks:
  - **I'm coming** / "One tap, so there's enough dough. That's all it does."
  - **Something to grow or make?** / "The shelf fills with local hands. Tell us at the table, or here."
  - **An idea for the place?** / "A maker day, the kids' corner, something we haven't thought of."
- Keep the footer: "Free. We count the pizza by hand."

### What this is for
- **Was:** "20 June is where we find out who's in. Come see if you are."
- **Now:** "We hold the shelf for now. One day the town won't need us to. Come walk through it,
  that's the only ask." (handover, not conversion)

### Stay close (newsletter)
- Keep the restraint already there: "one note before each gathering, never more." Reframe the
  heading from "hear about the next one" toward "we'll only ever send a note before the gate opens
  again." The quietest channel stays the quietest channel.

## What this protects
The other instance is about to build the GHL funnel. This doc is the conscience that rides next to
the plumbing: the page is welcome, the capture is small and underneath, and nobody who simply comes
and eats and yarns is a failed conversion. Build the workflows so they honour that. A neighbour who
never sells a thing on the shelf is the whole point.
