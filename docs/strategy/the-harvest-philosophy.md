## 1. The thread is already in the ground

This place did not start empty. The Blackall Range was timber country first, then dairy country, the co-operative dairy that built the towns, and Maleny became one of the most genuinely co-operative places in Australia: a food co-op, a credit union, community-owned things, people who learned to do hard work together because there was no other way.[^heritage] Art grew in the same soil. And this site was The Green Harvest, a nursery people came to for decades, part of that same instinct: hands in the ground, things grown and made locally, passed between neighbours.[^green-harvest]

So The Harvest is not a new idea dropped on a paddock. It is the next chapter of a thread that runs through this exact place: timber, dairy, co-op, art, nursery. The thread is people making things from the land, together, and keeping the value here. That is the philosophy, and it is not invented. We are picking up something the place already knows how to do.

## 2. The one message

Everything reduces to this: connection and community, made real by making things together. Not connection as a feeling, connection as a practice. You belong because you grew something, made something, mended something, fed someone. The dairy co-op understood that. The shop closing broke it. The Harvest is trying to rebuild the practice, not just the sentiment.

## 3. The Harvest as the place where the bigger model becomes real

**The relationship is already official, not a stretch.** The Harvest is described as the moment A Curious Tractor stopped being a mobile design practice and became a place-based organisation: ground to stand on, literally. It is named as both a public-facing community business and core operational infrastructure for the wider work, retreats, R&D, and community programs.[^place-based] So "The Harvest is where the model becomes real" is the documented intent, not a flourish.

**Goods on Country is the strongest link, and it is concrete.** Goods makes near-indestructible household goods from 100% recycled plastic for remote communities who co-design them rather than receive them. The Year-1 goal is the first containerised manufacturing facility on Jinibara Country, the same Country as Witta.[^goods] The Harvest could host that flagship line: the workshop, the four to six local jobs, the recycled-plastic feedstock, making Witta the on-the-ground site for the on-country model.[^goods-inf]

**The "container production" idea is Goods' own model, not a separate project.** Self-contained shipping-container production units, around 1,000 to 1,500 goods a year each, four to six local workers, manufacturing infrastructure communities own and operate.[^goods] One thing to keep straight: there is a different project, CONTAINED, a three-container experiential justice installation, easy to confuse with Goods' production containers. It gives a second real tie though: CONTAINED's permanent "Contained Wall" artefact is already planned to live at The Harvest.[^contained]

**JusticeHub Centre of Excellence is a live opportunity, not yet claimed.** It is a planned knowledge body: research translation, professional development and training that is lived-experience-informed and culturally safe, evidence building for youth-justice workers and policymakers. It has no documented physical site.[^justicehub] So The Harvest is the obvious unclaimed home for its training and residencies, since the site already hosts retreats and R&D. This is an opportunity to claim deliberately, not a done deal.[^justicehub-inf]

**The Farm is the Harvest's sister, upstream.** Land-based healing and cultural connection: conservation, residencies, the Black Cockatoo Valley arc. Officially the Harvest's place-cluster sister, separate books, same logic.[^farm] It is the land-and-food source the Harvest's making story runs downstream from.

**The economic spine, so this stays honest.** Every dollar in the wider organisation lands in one of four lanes: To Us, so the two humans holding it can keep showing up; To Down, clearing the past debts and receivables; To Grow, reinvesting into the projects including The Harvest; To Others, giving out through the charity, fellowships, and community grants.[^lanes] The governing rule is that each project earns the right to grow on its own revenue, and the Harvest's money funds the Harvest's growth. So the café, shop, and residency layers are not bolt-ons. They are how The Harvest earns its place in the To Grow lane.

**The synthesis.** The documented facts already make The Harvest the place. Goods (real, Jinibara Country) and the Contained Wall (real, planned on site) are the concrete ties today. JusticeHub's Centre of Excellence and the broader idea of Witta as the showroom for the on-country model are live opportunities to claim, not things to assert as done.

## 4. The phases are deepening, not pivoting

This is the key to not losing the soul as it grows. Each phase is the same idea at a new depth, never a swerve.

- **Now: the commons, free.** Garden, events, art space. Work days, the members' day, the shop forming. Build the people and the belonging. No commercial ask.
- **Next: moving inside.** The café and the restaurant, where food becomes a daily gathering, not an event. Sublicenced, someone runs it well, but carrying the same message: local, made here, honest about who made it and what they got paid.[^operating] The shop and the kitchen are the dairy-co-op instinct in a modern form.
- **Then: artists in residence.** The art space stops being a room and becomes a practice: artists living and making on site, the co-design ethos made permanent. Art was always in this soil; the residency makes it ongoing.
- **Underneath all of it: the co-op question.** The deepest version is whether some of these layers become genuinely community-owned or co-operative, not just community-feeling. That is the Maleny lineage taken seriously. Worth holding as a real possibility, not deciding now.

The discipline, in Nic's words: each layer has to be one clear thing, not a bit of everything. The café is a café done well. The residency is a residency done well. The thread connects them. It does not blur them.

## 5. How the layers earn their way

So this stays grounded and not just inspiring. The free community layer is funded as foundation-building. Year one is planned to run at a loss, covered, not pretending to break even.[^operating] The café, restaurant, shop, and residencies are the layers that earn revenue and, under the lease, share it. The wider projects that land here, Goods, JusticeHub, the container line, each carry their own revenue story; The Harvest gives them a place and takes a fair return for being that place.

The balance is the whole game: the commons is the gift, and the commercial layers are how the gift sustains itself. That is the thing to protect as it grows.
