## Activation: making the day land

### The arc of the day

The frame. This is a members' day, not a launch party. It is the place shown as it is right now, nothing polished, nothing performative (Harvest Note 02 sets exactly this tone). The site carries the Green Harvest nursery lineage, so the day is a return of a place to its people, not a grand opening. Build, events and art space are the three rooms; the day lets a guest meet all three by walking the rows. Free on the night (locked decision) is the whole posture: nobody is sold anything, so nobody has to perform a transaction. They just come and are in it.

Two parts, one day (locked).

Part one, 10:00 to 2:00, the maker session (Nic-led). This is the co-design ethos made literal: the people who are building the place get their hands on it before anyone else. The art space gets made by artists, the kids area gets made by kids. The morning is for makers and doers shaping the parts still forming. It is small (B1 18 seats), close, and working, not a demo. It lands well when the morning crew leave having actually shaped something, not watched a presentation. Nic holds the shape of the day as host and runs this session, so the morning carries one clear voice. Extra hands matter here so Nic is not solo (T32/T33 gate: Kurtis + partner and the Holland contact flagged, otherwise (unassigned, gate)).

The hinge, 1:30 to 2:00. The oven is fired at 1:30, the prep bar is set, drinks come out, B2 check-in opens at 1:45. The morning's quiet making rolls straight into the afternoon's gathering with no dead air. The maker session people stay on into the dinner, so the room fills from the inside out. That is what makes it feel like a community, not an audience.

Part two, 2:00 to late, the community gathering and make-your-own pizza. Everyone is invited, it runs into the evening. Make-your-own on set bases (three types: margherita, roast veg and feta, salami; gluten-free base and dairy-free cheese options) is the co-design ethos at the dinner table: guests build their own, the pizza lead runs the oven and the queue. Food from the garden and local makers ties the meal back to the shelf the shop is becoming. BYO alcohol, water, tea and coffee, a few soft drinks. The kids area runs supervised low-risk play through the afternoon, sited away from the oven keep-clear zone. At dusk the lights come on, the fire safety check happens, and the night winds down with leftovers offered first to guests to take home, then to the crew.

The close as part of the experience. The day ends the way it ran: leftovers handled with care (edible food offered out, scraps to on-site compost, packaging recycled), surfaces wiped, fire fully out and cold, lock up. The respect for the place at close is the same respect shown when the gate opened.

### The member-first reveal logic

The whole reveal is built so members feel chosen, not marketed at. Makers and doers hear the date first (Wk4 Makers' invite, "you are hearing this first"), then general members (Harvest Note 02, Tue 9 Jun, "you are hearing this before anyone else"). The public never gets "come 20 June"; the public sees the after-story (posts I and J, the thank-you email, the Empathy Ledger article). So the reveal does work the day cannot do for itself: it makes the morning crew feel like founders of the place and the afternoon crowd feel like insiders, which is exactly the co-design feeling the build is trying to seed.

### What makes it land

- The morning makers are still there when the afternoon opens, so the room is warm before the first newcomer arrives.
- Nothing is sold, so nothing feels like a pitch. The shop is shown as a forming idea, not a checkout.
- The space is shown taking shape, not finished, which matches every email and social post and keeps the promise honest.
- One named person captures the day with consent (Content + photos role, currently (unassigned, gate)) so the after-story exists for the public proof.
- Concrete care at the close (compost, leftovers offered out, fire cold) tells the community this is a place that is looked after.
