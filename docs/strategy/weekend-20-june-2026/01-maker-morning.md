## The event

Saturday 20 June 2026. A private maker session, **10:30 to 12:45**, finishing
before the public gate opens at **1pm**. 9 Gumland Drive, Witta QLD 4552, Jinibara
Country. Invite only, by personal ask, never broadcast.

This is the first time the maker community is in the room together. The Witta
Market runs that morning, so makers come straight from the market or from home.
The point is simple: makers meet each other, walk the place, and leave wanting to
put something on the shared shelf. Nothing is sold this morning. Nothing is
promised. The shelf is taking shape and these are the people closest to it.

The site was a plant nursery called The Green Harvest for decades. The Harvest
carries that name forward. That lineage is worth saying out loud to makers, it is
why a shared shelf here is not a new idea, it is an old one starting again.

## The shape of the morning

- **10:30 to 11:00. Arrive and a cup of tea.** Makers come in off the market.
  Nic and Susie greet each one by name at the door. Tea, coffee, water. No agenda
  yet, just hellos and warmth.
- **11:00 to 11:20. Welcome and the why.** A short Welcome to Country if a person
  is there to give it, otherwise Nic acknowledges Jinibara Country plainly. Then
  Nic tells the room what this is: a first gather, the Green Harvest lineage, a
  shared shelf for what people around Witta and Maleny grow and make. Honest
  signage, the maker named on the shelf. Not a finished shop.
- **11:20 to 12:00. Walk the place.** Nic walks makers through the gardens, the
  rammed earth building, the pavilion under the pecans, and where the shelf will
  sit. Makers see the room their work would live in. They start talking to each
  other, which is the real point.
- **12:00 to 12:30. The shelf conversation, 1:1.** Susie and Nic move person to
  person. What do you grow or make. Would you put something on the shelf. Each
  yes is captured by hand and pointed to the `/shop` form. This is a quiet
  conversation, not a sign-up table.
- **12:30 to 12:45. Close and hand over to the afternoon.** Thank the room. Tell
  makers they are welcome to stay on through the public afternoon (gate 1pm, pizza
  from 5pm, the day runs to dark). Clear the maker signage before the gate opens.

Free. No register, no sale, no Square. Kids welcome, there is a corner with paper
and chalk being set for the afternoon.

## Capture for the morning

The morning is the start of the shop shelf, so the written record is the whole
point. Capture is 1:1 and warm, never a sign-up sheet, never a broadcast.

How a maker is welcomed and captured:

- **Greet by name.** Susie and Nic know who is coming. Say hello like a neighbour,
  no pitch.
- **Listen for the energy.** A maker who says "I'd put my eggs (or jam, or mugs)
  on your shelf" is a producer. Catch it.
- **Tell them the honest offer.** Makers keep most of what you pay, the shop keeps
  a small share to cover card fees and overheads, and we name you on the shelf.
  The maker keeps ownership of the item until it sells. Low risk for them. Do not
  state a percent yet, the exact split inside 75 to 80 percent is held until
  Standard Ledger confirms (gate below).
- **Point each yes to the `/shop` form, 1:1.** Hand them the form link, or the
  card. This lands them at New interest in The Shop pipeline. Never put the form
  on a public surface for this morning, and never bulk-message makers about it.
- **Write it on the come-back sheet.** Name, what they grow or make, a way to
  reach them. The Monday sweep types it in.

`interest:markets` + `role:supplier` + `project:act-hv` and lands the contact at
New interest in The Shop pipeline (`Pdtr1ZIOvg3LrMSeNvHe`). A maker who is also

## Experience ideas (make it warm)

- **Greet every maker by name at the door.** Susie and Nic know the list. A name
  at the door does more than any sign.
- **A real cup of tea, not a paper-cup handout.** Slow the first twenty minutes
  down. Makers came off a market morning.
- **Introduce makers to each other.** "You make honey, she makes soap, you two
  should talk." The makers meeting each other is the morning's real work.
- **Show them the actual shelf spot.** Standing where their work would live makes
  it real in a way a pitch cannot.
- **Tell the Green Harvest story on the walk.** The site was a nursery called The
  Green Harvest for decades. This shelf is an old idea starting again.
- **Leave them with one small thing to bring.** Not a sign-up, an invitation: "next
  time you make a batch, bring one and we will find it a spot." A habit, not a
  booking.

## Risks and the fallback

- **Few makers come.** This is the main risk for a quiet, invite-only morning.
  **Fallback:** if it is three or four people, do not run it as a session at all.
  Run it as a slow cup of tea and a walk for whoever is there. The 1:1 shelf
  conversation works at any size, and a small warm room beats a thin staged one.
  The off-day maker outreach (warm leads first: Leeza, Rebecca, Monita, Lachie,
  then Witta and Wootha neighbours) carries the shelf either way, so this morning
  does not have to deliver a count.
- **Makers arrive late off the market.** Hold the Welcome until the room is mostly
  in, but do not push past 11:20, the walk needs its time before the gate.
- **A maker wants to sell on the day.** Nothing is sold on 20 June. Tell them
  honestly: today is a first gather, the shelf comes after. Point them to the
  `/shop` form for the real conversation.
- **The morning runs long into the public gate.** Hard stop at 12:45. Clear maker
  signage and reset before 1pm. The afternoon is a different moment.
- **A photo of a maker who has not said yes.** Do not take it. Consent first,
  every time. In a town of about 1,300 people, an unconsented photo costs trust
  you cannot rebuild.
